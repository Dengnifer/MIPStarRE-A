\section{Key theorem forms}

For an admissible tuple $(q,m,d)$ and a strategy that wins the Pauli basis test
with probability at least $1-\eps$, each subtest has conditional failure
$O(\eps)$.  In particular, the Magic Square average is conditioned on
\[
  \gamma(\omega)
  =\tr\big((r_X\ind_m(u_X))\cdot(r_Z\ind_m(u_Z))\big)\ne0,
\]
and the low-degree line-evaluation family is completed on zero-direction lines
by an additional outcome.  The observable consequence is
\[
  X^{r_X}(u_X)Z^{r_Z}(u_Z)
  \approx_{\sqrt\eps}
  (-1)^{\gamma(\omega)}Z^{r_Z}(u_Z)X^{r_X}(u_X)
\]
on average over all tuples.  These statements hold for every admissible
parameter tuple; no assumption $6md\leq q$ is needed.

\section{The source assertions and discrepancies}

The source states the measurement implications at lines~197--265 and the
observable implications at lines~267--362 of the Pauli analysis
\cite[lines~197--362]{Ji2020MIPStar}.%
\footnote{See
\path{references/qpbt-paper/14_analysis_of_the_pauli_basis_test.tex}.}
Four corrections are required.

First, the displayed conditioning event for the Magic Square average omits
$\ind_m$ and writes the scalar expression
$\tr((r_Xu_X)\cdot(r_Zu_Z))$, even though $u_X,u_Z\in\F_q^m$ and the phase
used by the game is defined through vectors in $\F_q^{2^m}$.

Second, the proof assumes $6md\leq q$ and disposes of the complementary range
by appealing to the final Pauli soundness theorem, whose proof uses the lemma
under discussion.  That appeal cannot prove the lemma as an unrestricted
standalone result.  The corrected exact probability calculation gives
universal lower bounds for both conditioning events whenever the parameters
are admissible, and removes this circular reduction.%
\footnote{The probability correction is documented separately in
\path{docs/paper-gaps/qpbt_anticommuting-probability.tex}.}

Third, the source treats evaluation classes of a line measurement as a POVM
indexed by $\F_q$.  A zero-direction line admits nonconstant polynomial
representatives with no well-defined value on its singleton image, so these
classes form only a sub-measurement.  Adding an outcome $\bot$ for the missing
mass makes the consistency relation well typed; the point family is completed
by a zero effect at the same outcome.

Finally, the observable proof writes
$Z^{r_X}(u_X)X^{r_Z}(u_Z)$ where the intended reversed product is
$Z^{r_Z}(u_Z)X^{r_X}(u_X)$.  The source label prefix
\path{len:qld-win-implications-obs} is likewise a typographical error for
\path{lem:qld-win-implications-obs}.

\section{The corrected argument}

Every question type and every relevant subtest has a universal positive
sampling weight.  For admissible parameters, $1\leq m\leq q$, and the exact
calculation of the anticommuting probability gives probability at least
$2^{-4}$ for the anticommuting event and at least $1/2$ for its complement.
Conditioning therefore enlarges rejection probabilities only by a universal
factor.  The measurement implications follow directly from their rejection
events, including the completed zero-direction outcome.  Data processing and
the agreement identities yield the observable relations; the corrected Magic
Square phase and reversed product then give the displayed signed commutation
law.

\section{Conclusion}

The winning implications remain valid for all admissible parameters, but the
printed proof does not establish that scope and several formulas need local
correction.  Exact conditioning probabilities, the explicit missing outcome,
and the corrected phase and operator indices give the form used by the Pauli
basis analysis.
