\section{Key theorem forms}

The paper considers a role-symmetric Magic Square game: the verifier samples a
constraint-variable incidence and chooses uniformly which player receives the
constraint.  The printed rigidity statement quantifies over every strategy
$\strategy=(\ket\psi,A,B)$ for this game.  The corrected statement instead
assumes that $\strategy=(\ket\psi,M)$ is symmetric, so the state is invariant
under exchanging the two players and both players use the same measurement
family $M$.  If this strategy wins with probability at least $1-\eps$, then the
same local-isometry, state-extraction, variable-measurement, and
anticommutation conclusions hold with error $O(\sqrt\eps)$.

The Pauli analysis does not assume that its ambient strategy is symmetric, but
it needs only the one-sided anticommutation consequences.  Those consequences
remain valid for an arbitrary strategy: apply the cited one-way self-test
separately to the two role orientations.  The corrected blueprint records this
as a separate lemma rather than applying the full symmetric-strategy theorem
outside its domain.

\section{The source assertion}

The theorem labelled \path{thm:ms-rigidity} in
\cite[Section~8.2]{Ji2020MIPStar} begins with an arbitrary strategy for the
role-symmetric game and concludes that local isometries extract two EPR pairs,
that both players' variable measurements are close to the corresponding ideal
Pauli measurements, and that the selected variables approximately
anticommute.%
\footnote{See
\path{references/qpbt-paper/08_classical_and_quantum_low_degree_tests.tex},
lines~620--652.  The standing symmetric-strategy convention is in
\path{references/qpbt-paper/06_nonlocal_games_and_mipstar.tex}, lines~166--176,
and the use in the Pauli analysis is in
\path{references/qpbt-paper/14_analysis_of_the_pauli_basis_test.tex},
lines~333--350.}

The cited result of Coladangelo and Stark is a self-test for the usual one-way
linear-constraint-system game: Alice receives an equation and Bob receives a
variable.  Its strategy has equation-indexed observables for Alice and one
variable observable for Bob.  It therefore does not, by itself, constrain the
measurements used after the two roles are reversed.%
\footnote{See \path{references/cs-paper/self-testing.tex}, lines~669--685, and
\path{references/cs-paper/specific-games.tex}, lines~111--135.}

\section{A perfect two-copy counterexample}

Start with the ideal perfect strategy for the one-way Magic Square game and
share two independent copies of its state.  When Alice receives the constraint,
the players answer from copy~1: Alice uses the constraint measurement and Bob
uses the matching variable measurement.  When Bob receives the constraint,
they answer from copy~2 with the roles reversed.  Each orientation is an ideal
one-way strategy, so the role-symmetric game is won with probability one.

For a fixed variable, however, Alice's variable observable acts on copy~2 and
Bob's acts on copy~1.  Their outcomes are independent and unbiased.  Thus the
cross-player observable correlation is $0$, whereas the corresponding ideal
measurements on an extracted EPR pair have correlation $1$.  At $\eps=0$ the
printed rigidity conclusions require exact state and measurement identities,
which would preserve this correlation.  The two-copy strategy therefore
refutes the arbitrary-strategy statement.

\section{Correction and blueprint/formalization status}

For a symmetric strategy the two orientations use the same measurements and
have equal success probability.  The value of their uniform mixture is thus
the value of either one-way orientation, so the cited one-way rigidity theorem
applies.  Symmetry also identifies the variable families that the two-copy
counterexample separated.  This gives a valid correction of the full
simultaneous extraction statement.

For the weaker conclusion used in the Pauli analysis, no symmetry hypothesis
is necessary.  In an arbitrary strategy, split the success probability by
orientation.  Each oriented game has failure probability at most twice the
failure probability of their uniform mixture.  Applying one-way rigidity with
Alice and Bob in their two respective roles shows that each player's two
variable observables approximately anticommute on the shared state, with the
same $O(\sqrt\eps)$ scale after changing the universal constant.

The blueprint now states \path{thm:ms-rigidity} for a symmetric strategy, adds
\path{lem:ms-one-sided-anticommutation} for the arbitrary-strategy consequence,
and routes the Pauli proof through the latter.  The current formal declaration
still quantifies over an arbitrary strategy and asserts the false simultaneous
extraction conclusion.  Its hypothesis is therefore too weak for the corrected
full theorem.  No Lean declaration yet states the valid one-sided consequence;
both formal repairs are tracked in \ghissue{172}.%
\footnote{The current declaration is \leanid{exists\_ms\_rigidity} in
\doclink{MIPStarRE/QPBT/Test/MagicSquareTheorems.lean}.}

\section{Conclusion}

The arbitrary-strategy simultaneous rigidity theorem for the role-symmetric
Magic Square game is false.  The corrected full theorem assumes a symmetric
strategy, which blocks the two-copy orientation counterexample.  The Pauli
basis analysis does not need that restriction: its one-sided anticommutation
claim follows for arbitrary strategies by treating the two orientations
separately.
