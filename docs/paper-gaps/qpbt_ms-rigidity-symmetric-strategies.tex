\section{At a glance}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Difficulty: easy to diagnose, medium to repair.}  The printed
  theorem is false: a perfect strategy for the Magic Square game may answer the
  two orientations of a constraint--variable question pair on two independent
  maximally entangled copies, and then the two players' variable observables
  are uncorrelated where the conclusion forces them to agree.  Restricting to
  symmetric strategies, the source's standing convention, does \emph{not}
  remove the counterexample; the property the conclusion actually needs is
  the agreement of the two players' $\Variable_1$ and $\Variable_5$
  measurements on the shared state, that is, their consistency.  The adopted
  correction takes that agreement, measured by a defect $\delta$, as a
  hypothesis and replaces $\sqrt\eps$ by $\sqrt\eps + \sqrt\delta$ in the
  conclusion; symmetric consistent strategies are the case $\delta = 0$.
  \item \textbf{Estimated weight:} the statement repair is done: one
  definition (the consistency defect), the restated theorem, its
  specialization to symmetric consistent strategies, and the transfer of the
  hypothesis to the reflections of the projective dilation, about
  \textbf{350 lines} in total.  Proving the repaired theorem is the remaining
  task, about \textbf{3--4 interfaces} (the second logical qubit pair, the
  joint swap-isometry extraction of the two EPR pairs from the anticommutation
  and the cross-player agreement, and the post-isometry transfers), on the
  order of \textbf{one to two thousand lines}.
  \item \textbf{Mathlib/project split:} approximately \textbf{10\% Mathlib} and
  \textbf{90\% project-local}.  Mathlib supplies finite-dimensional inner
  product spaces, Kronecker products, and isometries; games, strategies,
  values, consistency, the Naimark dilation, the Magic Square solution-group
  computation and the swap isometries are project-local.
  \item \textbf{Key Mathlib inputs:} finite-dimensional complex inner product
  spaces and their isometries, Kronecker products of matrices, and elementary
  trace identities for maximally entangled states.  The main inputs are the
  cited robust self-test of \cite{Coladangelo2017Robust} and the game and
  strategy vocabulary of \cite[Section~5]{Ji2020MIPStar}.  Tracked in
  \ghissue{172}; the refutation is \ghissue{105}, which also owns the
  remaining proof; the decision is item~B5 on \ghissue{26}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Key theorem forms}

Throughout, $\game^{\MS}$ is the Magic Square game in the role-symmetric
presentation of the source, $\strategy$ a tensor product strategy for it,
$\eps \ge 0$ an error parameter, and $\approx_\zeta$ the state-dependent
approximation recalled in the next section.  Four statements must be kept
apart.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Source theorem form.}  For every strategy
  $\strategy = (\ket{\psi}, A, B)$ with
  $\val^*(\game^{\MS}, \strategy) = 1-\eps$ there are local isometries
  $\phi_{\mathrm A}, \phi_{\mathrm B}$ and an auxiliary state $\ket{\aux}$ such
  that $\phi_{\mathrm A} \ot \phi_{\mathrm B} \ket{\psi}$ is $O(\sqrt\eps)$-close
  to $\ket{\EPR_2}^{\ot 2} \ot \ket{\aux}$ and \emph{both} players'
  $\Variable_1$ and $\Variable_5$ measurements are $\sqrt\eps$-close to the
  ideal $\sigma^X$ and $\sigma^Z$ measurements on the extracted qubits.
  \item \textbf{Cited theorem form.}  The robust self-test of
  \cite[Theorem~6.9]{Coladangelo2017Robust} for a linear constraint system
  game, in which one player always receives a constraint and the other always
  receives a variable.  Its conclusion compares the \emph{constraint} player's
  observables with an ideal representation and the \emph{variable} player's
  observables with its conjugate.  It says nothing about the constraint
  player's variable observables, which that game never asks for.
  \item \textbf{Refuted intermediate form.}  The source theorem form with
  $\strategy$ assumed symmetric, $\strategy = (\ket{\psi}, M)$.  Section~%
  \ref{sec:symmetric} exhibits a symmetric strategy of value $1$ violating the
  conclusion, so this form is false as well.
  \item \textbf{Corrected form (adopted).}  Let $\eps, \delta \ge 0$ and let
  $\strategy = (\ket{\psi}, A, B)$ be a strategy with
  $\val^*(\game^{\MS}, \strategy) \ge 1-\eps$ whose $\Variable_1$ and
  $\Variable_5$ measurements agree between the players up to $\delta$,
  \begin{equation}
    \Delta_j(\strategy) :=
    \sum_{b \in \F_2} \bigl\| (A^{\Variable_j}_b \ot \Id
      - \Id \ot B^{\Variable_j}_b) \ket{\psi} \bigr\|^2 \le \delta,
    \qquad j \in \{1, 5\}.
    \label{eq:defect}
  \end{equation}
  Then the conclusion of the source theorem form holds with $\sqrt\eps$
  replaced by $\eta = \sqrt\eps + \sqrt\delta$ throughout: the state is
  $O(\eta)$-close to $\ket{\EPR_2}^{\ot 2} \ot \ket{\aux}$, the four
  measurement relations and the two anticommutation consequences hold with
  $\approx_\eta$.  A symmetric strategy whose $\Variable_1$ and $\Variable_5$
  measurements are consistent on $\ket{\psi}$ has $\Delta_1 = \Delta_5 = 0$,
  so the class of symmetric consistent strategies fixed by the owner decision
  is the case $\delta = 0$, in which the conclusion is the source's display
  verbatim.  Symmetry is not assumed: the proof does not use it, and the
  source's application does not have it in the form the formalization needs
  (Section~\ref{sec:sufficiency}).
\end{enumerate}
The formal declaration carrying the corrected form is
\leanid{MIPStarRE.QPBT.exists\_ms\_rigidity}, with the witness and distance
definitions \leanid{MIPStarRE.QPBT.MsRigidityWitness},
\leanid{MIPStarRE.QPBT.msOperatorDistanceA},
\leanid{MIPStarRE.QPBT.msOperatorDistanceB}, and
\leanid{MIPStarRE.QPBT.msAnticommutatorDistanceA} in
\doclink{MIPStarRE/QPBT/Test/MagicSquareTheorems.lean}, and the defect
\eqref{eq:defect} as \leanid{MIPStarRE.QPBT.msVariableConsistencyDefect} in
\doclink{MIPStarRE/QPBT/Test/MagicSquareTheorems/Rigidity/Consistency.lean}.
The specialization to symmetric consistent strategies is
\leanid{MIPStarRE.QPBT.exists\_ms\_rigidity\_of\_symmetric\_consistent}.

\section{The source assertion}

The Magic Square game $\game^{\MS}$ of \cite[Section~7]{Ji2020MIPStar} has nine
variables $x_1,\ldots,x_9$ over $\F_2$ in a $3\times 3$ grid and six parity
constraints, one per row and column.  Its verifier samples a uniformly random
edge $\Constraint_i \,\text{---}\, \Variable_j$ of the bipartite incidence
graph and sends one endpoint to a uniformly random player and the other
endpoint to the other player.  The constraint player answers with an
assignment to the three variables of its constraint, the variable player with
one bit, and the players win when the assignment satisfies the constraint and
the two reported values of $x_j$ agree.  Two features are used below: the game
is \emph{role-symmetric}, so each player is asked constraint questions and
variable questions, and its question distribution is supported on
constraint--variable pairs, so the same question is never sent to both
players.

A strategy $\strategy = (\ket{\psi}, A, B)$ consists of a unit vector
$\ket{\psi} \in \mH_{\mathrm A} \ot \mH_{\mathrm B}$ and POVMs $\{A^x_a\}$,
$\{B^y_b\}$; $\val^*(\game^{\MS},\strategy)$ is the acceptance probability.
The strategy is \emph{symmetric} when $\mH_{\mathrm A} = \mH_{\mathrm B}$,
$\ket{\psi}$ is invariant under exchanging the two factors, and $A = B$; the
source writes such a strategy $\strategy = (\ket{\psi}, M)$ and states that it
almost always restricts attention to this class.%
\footnote{Definitions and the convention:
\path{references/qpbt-paper/06_nonlocal_games_and_mipstar.tex}, lines~74--92
and~174.  In this repository: \path{def:symmetric-game} in
\path{blueprint/src/chapter/ch12_qpbt_games.tex}, formalized as
\leanid{MIPStarRE.QPBT.SymmetricStrategy}.}
For operators $M, N$ and a state $\ket{\varphi}$ we write
$M \approx_\zeta N$ for
$\bra{\varphi}(M-N)^\dagger(M-N)\ket{\varphi} \le O(\zeta)$, and for POVM
families $\{M_a\}, \{N_a\}$ for
$\sum_a \bra{\varphi}(M_a-N_a)^\dagger(M_a-N_a)\ket{\varphi} \le O(\zeta)$;
the state is named wherever the relation is used.

The rigidity theorem asserts:%
\footnote{Statement and proof at
\path{references/qpbt-paper/08_classical_and_quantum_low_degree_tests.tex},
lines~620--652; the blueprint statement is \path{thm:ms-rigidity} in
\path{blueprint/src/chapter/ch13_qpbt_test.tex}, now in the corrected form.
Tracked in \ghissue{172}; the refutation is recorded on \ghissue{105}, and the
decisions to record and to repair the statement are items~B4 and~B5 on
\ghissue{26}.}
if $\strategy = (\ket{\psi},A,B)$ succeeds in $\game^{\MS}$ with probability
$1-\eps$, then there are local isometries
$\phi_{\mathrm A} \colon \mH_{\mathrm A} \to \mH_{\mathrm A'} \ot
\mH_{\mathrm A''}$ and
$\phi_{\mathrm B} \colon \mH_{\mathrm B} \to \mH_{\mathrm B'} \ot
\mH_{\mathrm B''}$ with
$\mH_{\mathrm A'}, \mH_{\mathrm B'} \cong (\C^2)^{\ot 2}$, and a state
$\ket{\aux} \in \mH_{\mathrm A''} \ot \mH_{\mathrm B''}$, such that
\begin{equation}
  \bigl\| \phi_{\mathrm A} \ot \phi_{\mathrm B}\ket{\psi}
    - \ket{\EPR_2}^{\ot 2} \ot \ket{\aux} \bigr\| \le O(\sqrt\eps)
  \tag{\path{thm:ms-rigidity}, 1}
\end{equation}
and, writing $\tilde A^x_a = \phi_{\mathrm A} A^x_a \phi_{\mathrm A}^\dagger$
and $\tilde B^y_b = \phi_{\mathrm B} B^y_b \phi_{\mathrm B}^\dagger$,
\begin{equation}
  \tilde A^{\Variable_1}_b \ot \Id \approx_{\sqrt\eps}
    (\sigma^X_b)_{\mathrm A'} \ot \Id,
  \qquad
  \Id \ot \tilde B^{\Variable_1}_b \approx_{\sqrt\eps}
    \Id \ot (\sigma^X_b)_{\mathrm B'},
  \tag{\path{thm:ms-rigidity}, 2}
\end{equation}
with respect to $\ket{\Theta} = \ket{\EPR_2}^{\ot 2}_{\mathrm{A'B'}} \ot
\ket{\aux}_{\mathrm{A''B''}}$, together with the two companion relations for
$\Variable_5$ and $\sigma^Z$ and the anticommutation consequence for the
associated observables.

\section{What the conclusion forces}
\label{sec:forces}

Write $O_A = \tilde A^{\Variable_1}_0 - \tilde A^{\Variable_1}_1$ and
$O_B = \tilde B^{\Variable_1}_0 - \tilde B^{\Variable_1}_1$ for the transported
$\pm 1$ observables, and $\mathcal O_A, \mathcal O_B$ for the untransported
ones.  Take $\eps = 0$, so that all the estimates above are exact.  Then
$\phi_{\mathrm A} \ot \phi_{\mathrm B}\ket{\psi} = \ket{\Theta}$, and since
$\phi^\dagger \phi = \Id$ for an isometry, $\tilde M \phi = \phi M$ for each
transported operator, whence
\begin{equation}
  \bra{\psi}\,\mathcal O_A \ot \mathcal O_B\,\ket{\psi}
  = \bra{\Theta}\, O_A \ot O_B \,\ket{\Theta}
  = \bra{\Theta}\, \sigma^X_{\mathrm A'} \ot \sigma^X_{\mathrm B'}
    \,\ket{\Theta}
  = 1 .
  \label{eq:forced-correlation}
\end{equation}
The middle equality uses the exact form of the two relations in
(\path{thm:ms-rigidity},~2) and the self-adjointness of the observables; the
last one is the identity
$\bra{\EPR_2}\, M \ot N \,\ket{\EPR_2} = \tr(M N^{\mathsf T})/2$ applied to
$M = N = \sigma^X$ on the first extracted qubit pair.  For $\pm 1$ observables,
$\bra{\psi} \mathcal O_A \ot \mathcal O_B \ket{\psi} = 1$ is equivalent to
$(\mathcal O_A \ot \Id)\ket{\psi} = (\Id \ot \mathcal O_B)\ket{\psi}$, that is,
to the two players' $\Variable_1$ measurements agreeing on $\ket{\psi}$.  The
conclusion of the theorem therefore contains a cross-player agreement
assertion about a question pair that the game never samples.

\section{A perfect strategy violating the conclusion}
\label{sec:counterexample}

Fix the canonical Mermin--Peres operator solution on
$\mathcal K = \C^2 \ot \C^2$: the nine $\pm 1$ observables
\[
  \begin{array}{ccc}
    \sigma^X \ot \Id & \Id \ot \sigma^X & \sigma^X \ot \sigma^X \\
    \Id \ot \sigma^Z & \sigma^Z \ot \Id & \sigma^Z \ot \sigma^Z \\
    \sigma^X \ot \sigma^Z & \sigma^Z \ot \sigma^X & \sigma^Y \ot \sigma^Y
  \end{array}
\]
placed in the grid, whose rows and first two columns multiply to $\Id$ and
whose third column multiplies to $-\Id$.  All nine are real and symmetric.  Let
$\ket{\Omega} \in \mathcal K \ot \mathcal K$ be the maximally entangled state,
which is $\ket{\EPR_2}^{\ot 2}$ after reordering the qubits, and let $M$ be the
measurement family that answers $\Constraint_i$ by jointly measuring the three
commuting observables of row or column $i$ and answers $\Variable_j$ by the
two-outcome measurement $\{(\Id \pm O_j)/2\}$.  Then $(\ket{\Omega}, M)$ is a
symmetric, projective, consistent strategy of value $1$.

Now give each player a copy of $\mathcal K \ot \mathcal K$ and split the two
roles across the two copies: on a constraint question the first player measures
copy~$1$ and the second measures copy~$2$, and on a variable question the first
player measures copy~$2$ and the second measures copy~$1$.  With the state
$\ket{\Omega}_{\mathrm A_1 \mathrm B_1} \ot
\ket{\Omega}_{\mathrm A_2 \mathrm B_2}$, every sampled question pair consists of
one constraint and one variable, so the two measurements actually performed are
the canonical perfect pair acting on a single maximally entangled copy: on
copy~$1$ when the first player holds the constraint, and on copy~$2$ when the
second player does.  The value is therefore $1$.  The two players' $\Variable_1$
observables, however, act on $\mathrm A_2$ and $\mathrm B_1$, whose joint
reduced state is a product of maximally mixed states, so
\begin{equation}
  \bra{\psi}\,\mathcal O_A \ot \mathcal O_B\,\ket{\psi}
  = \frac{\tr(\sigma^X \ot \Id)}{4} \cdot \frac{\tr(\sigma^X \ot \Id)}{4}
  = 0 ,
  \label{eq:zero-correlation}
\end{equation}
contradicting \eqref{eq:forced-correlation}.  The same computation with
$\sigma^Z$ refutes the $\Variable_5$ relations.  The source statement is thus
false at $\eps = 0$, and no choice of isometries or auxiliary state can repair
it, since \eqref{eq:forced-correlation} used only that $\phi_{\mathrm A}$ and
$\phi_{\mathrm B}$ are isometries.  In the terms of \eqref{eq:defect}, this
strategy has $\Delta_1 = \Delta_5 = 1$: for $\pm1$ observables
$\sum_b \|(A_b \ot \Id - \Id \ot B_b)\ket\psi\|^2
= 1 - \bra\psi \mathcal O_A \ot \mathcal O_B \ket\psi$
whenever the two measurements are projective on the state.

\section{Symmetry does not repair the statement}
\label{sec:symmetric}

The strategy just described is not symmetric: the two players use different
measurement families.  It is natural to hope that the source's standing
restriction to symmetric strategies excludes it.  It does not.

Give each player a flag qubit and two copies of $\mathcal K$, so that the local
space is $\C^2 \ot \mathcal K \ot \mathcal K$, and take the state
\[
  \ket{\psi} = \frac{1}{\sqrt2}
    \bigl( \ket{0}_{f_{\mathrm A}}\ket{1}_{f_{\mathrm B}}
         + \ket{1}_{f_{\mathrm A}}\ket{0}_{f_{\mathrm B}} \bigr)
    \ot \ket{\Omega}_{\mathrm A_1 \mathrm B_1}
    \ot \ket{\Omega}_{\mathrm A_2 \mathrm B_2} .
\]
Both players use the single family
\begin{align*}
  M^{\Constraint_i}_a
    &= \ket{0}\!\bra{0}_f \ot (M^{\Constraint_i}_a)_1 \ot \Id_2
     + \ket{1}\!\bra{1}_f \ot \Id_1 \ot (M^{\Constraint_i}_a)_2 , \\
  M^{\Variable_j}_b
    &= \ket{0}\!\bra{0}_f \ot \Id_1 \ot (M^{\Variable_j}_b)_2
     + \ket{1}\!\bra{1}_f \ot (M^{\Variable_j}_b)_1 \ot \Id_2 :
\end{align*}
a player whose flag reads $0$ answers constraints from copy~$1$ and variables
from copy~$2$, and a player whose flag reads $1$ does the opposite.  Since
$\ket{\Omega}$ is invariant under exchanging its two factors, $\ket{\psi}$ is
invariant under exchanging the two players' registers, and the two players use
identical measurements; the strategy is symmetric in the sense of the source.

The flags are perfectly anticorrelated, so on each branch exactly one player
plays the role assigned to flag~$0$.  On the branch $f_{\mathrm A} = 0$, if the
first player receives the constraint it measures copy~$1$ while the second
player, whose flag reads $1$, answers its variable question from copy~$1$ as
well; if the roles are exchanged, both act on copy~$2$.  In every case the pair
of measurements performed is the canonical perfect pair on one maximally
entangled copy, and the branch $f_{\mathrm A} = 1$ is its mirror image.  The
value is again $1$.  The correlation of the two players' $\Variable_1$
observables is, branch by branch, the product of two traces of $\sigma^X \ot
\Id$ against maximally mixed states, hence $0$ as in
\eqref{eq:zero-correlation}, and the cross terms between the two flag branches
vanish because the observables are flag-diagonal.  So
\eqref{eq:forced-correlation} fails for a symmetric strategy of value $1$,
and the same computation refutes the $\Variable_5$ relations.  Again
$\Delta_1 = \Delta_5 = 1$.%
\footnote{This strategy is the player-role symmetrization of the previous one,
so the symmetrization construction of \path{lem:symmetric-strat}
(\path{references/qpbt-paper/06_nonlocal_games_and_mipstar.tex}, lines~94--130)
does not repair the statement either: it is value-preserving and moves the two
role-dependent families into two blocks of one family, which is exactly what
the flag register does above.  The value, the swap invariance, and the two
vanishing correlations have also been verified numerically for the explicit
$32$-dimensional local spaces described here.}

\section{What the cited self-test provides}

The source imports the theorem from \cite[Theorem~6.9]{Coladangelo2017Robust},
whose notion of robust self-testing is stated for a linear constraint system
game in the ordinary one-way presentation: one player receives a vertex $v$,
that is a constraint, and answers with observables $A^{(v)}_e$ for the
variables $e$ occurring in it, while the other receives a variable $e$ and
answers with an observable $B_e$.%
\footnote{\path{references/cs-paper/self-testing.tex}, lines~669--689 for the
definition and lines~706--724 for the theorem; the Magic Square instance is at
\path{references/cs-paper/specific-games.tex}, lines~111--135.}
The conclusion compares $\phi_{\mathrm A} A^{(v)}_e \phi_{\mathrm A}^\dagger$
with an ideal representation $\tau(e)$ and
$\phi_{\mathrm B} B_e \phi_{\mathrm B}^\dagger$ with its conjugate.  In that
game the first player never receives a bare variable question, so the theorem
constrains one player's constraint observables and the other player's variable
observables, and nothing else.

The source relabels this conclusion for the role-symmetric game, in which each
player has both kinds of question, and asserts it for both players' variable
observables.  The step from the cited conclusion to the printed one is exactly
the cross-player agreement isolated in Section~\ref{sec:forces}, and the
strategies of Sections~\ref{sec:counterexample} and~\ref{sec:symmetric} show
that neither the value hypothesis nor symmetry supplies it.

\section{The correction}
\label{sec:repair}

Recall that a measurement $\{M_a\}$ is \emph{consistent} on a state
$\ket{\psi} \in \mH \ot \mH$ when
$(M_a \ot \Id)\ket{\psi} = (\Id \ot M_a)\ket{\psi}$ for every outcome $a$, and
that a strategy is consistent when all of its measurements are.%
\footnote{\path{references/qpbt-paper/06_nonlocal_games_and_mipstar.tex},
lines~144--174; \path{def:consistent-measurement} and
\path{def:consistent-strategy} in
\path{blueprint/src/chapter/ch12_qpbt_games.tex}, formalized as
\leanid{MIPStarRE.QPBT.Measurement.IsConsistentOn} and
\leanid{MIPStarRE.QPBT.IsConsistentStrategyOn}.}
For a symmetric strategy and a two-outcome measurement with observable
$\mathcal O = M_0 - M_1$, consistency gives
\[
  \bra{\psi}\, \mathcal O \ot \mathcal O \,\ket{\psi}
  = \bra{\psi}\, (\mathcal O \ot \Id)(\mathcal O \ot \Id) \,\ket{\psi}
  = \bra{\psi}\, \mathcal O^2 \ot \Id \,\ket{\psi} = 1 ,
\]
which is precisely the assertion \eqref{eq:forced-correlation} that the
conclusion forces.  Consistency is thus the missing hypothesis, and it is not an
artefact of the $\eps = 0$ analysis: it is the property that ties the untested
same-question pair to the tested constraint--variable pairs.

\paragraph{The adopted class.}
Owner decision B5 adopts the class of symmetric consistent strategies and
allows a further adjustment provided the corrected statement is both correct
and sufficient for the source's application.  Two observations shape the
adjustment.  First, the proof below uses consistency only for the two
distinguished variable measurements and only in the cross-player form
$(A^{\Variable_j}_b \ot \Id)\ket\psi \approx (\Id \ot B^{\Variable_j}_b)\ket\psi$;
it never uses that $\ket\psi$ is swap-invariant or that $A = B$.  Second, the
source's application (Section~\ref{sec:sufficiency}) does not have exact
consistency: it derives the agreement of the two variable measurements from
the consistency subtests of the Pauli basis test, up to an error of order
$\eps$, and its Lean counterpart is not a symmetric strategy at all.  The
statement is therefore taken in the robust form of item~4 of Section~2: an
arbitrary strategy whose defects \eqref{eq:defect} at $\Variable_1$ and
$\Variable_5$ are at most $\delta$, with $\sqrt\eps$ replaced by
$\eta = \sqrt\eps + \sqrt\delta$ in every estimate of the conclusion.  The
decided class is contained in it as the case $\delta = 0$: for a symmetric
strategy, $\Delta_j = 0$ is exactly the consistency of $\{M^{\Variable_j}_b\}$
on $\ket\psi$ (\leanid{MIPStarRE.QPBT.msVariableConsistencyDefect\_eq\_zero\_of\_isConsistent}),
and the conclusion is then the source's display verbatim.  The two
counterexamples have $\Delta_1 = \Delta_5 = 1$, so the hypothesis excludes
them, and at $\eps = \delta = 0$ the hypothesis is literally the assertion
\eqref{eq:forced-correlation} that the conclusion forces; there is no room
left for a counterexample of the same kind.

\paragraph{Derivation from the cited self-test.}
Let $\strategy = (\ket{\psi}, A, B)$ satisfy the hypotheses.  Conditioning on
the orientation in which the first player receives the constraint gives a
strategy for the one-way linear constraint system game of value at least
$1-2\eps$, and exactly $1-\eps$ if $\strategy$ is symmetric.  After a Naimark
dilation of both players' measurements, which does not change any Born
probability, \cite[Theorem~6.9]{Coladangelo2017Robust} applies to this
one-way strategy and yields $\phi_{\mathrm A}, \phi_{\mathrm B}, \ket\aux$ with
(\path{thm:ms-rigidity},~1) at scale $\sqrt\eps$ and with the relations for
the second player's variable observables, $\Id \ot \tilde B^{\Variable_1}_b
\approx_{\sqrt\eps} \Id \ot (\sigma^X_b)_{\mathrm B'}$ and likewise for
$\Variable_5$, after the local change of basis noted in the source.  (The
robust theorem is stated for projective measurements; the passage from the
dilated projections back to the original effects costs the leakage of the
dilated operators out of the range of the dilation, which is bounded by the
cell-consistency masses of a strategy of value $1-\eps$, as in the transfer
layer of the formalization.)  For the first player, the hypothesis gives
\[
  (\tilde A^{\Variable_j}_b \ot \Id)\,\phi\ket\psi
  = \phi\,(A^{\Variable_j}_b \ot \Id)\ket\psi
  \approx_{\sqrt\delta}
  \phi\,(\Id \ot B^{\Variable_j}_b)\ket\psi
  = (\Id \ot \tilde B^{\Variable_j}_b)\,\phi\ket\psi,
\]
where $\phi = \phi_{\mathrm A} \ot \phi_{\mathrm B}$ and $\approx_{\sqrt\delta}$
is closeness in norm; the second player's relation and
(\path{thm:ms-rigidity},~1) move the right-hand side to
$(\Id \ot \sigma^W_b)\ket\Theta$, and
$(\Id \ot \sigma^W_b)\ket{\EPR_2}^{\ot 2} = (\sigma^W_b \ot \Id)\ket{\EPR_2}^{\ot 2}$
because $\sigma^X_b$ and $\sigma^Z_b$ are real symmetric.  Every operator
involved is a contraction, so the first player's relations hold in norm at
scale $\sqrt\eps + \sqrt\delta$, hence as $\approx_\eta$ estimates of the
squared distance.  The anticommutation consequences follow from the four
relations as in the source: $(\tilde A^{\Variable_1}\tilde A^{\Variable_5}
\ot \Id)\ket\Theta$ is within $O(\eta)$ of
$(\sigma^X\sigma^Z \ot \Id)\ket\Theta$ by replacing the two factors one at a
time, the second replacement using the same transposition identity to move
$\sigma^Z$ to the other side where it commutes with $\tilde A^{\Variable_1}$;
the reversed product is within $O(\eta)$ of
$(\sigma^Z\sigma^X \ot \Id)\ket\Theta = -(\sigma^X\sigma^Z \ot \Id)\ket\Theta$.
Bounds of order $\eta$ in norm are bounds of order $\eta^2 \le 2(\eps+\delta)
\le 2\eta$ in the squared distance for $\eta \le 1$, and for $\eta > 1$ every
estimate is trivial, so one universal constant serves all parameters.

\paragraph{The formal route.}
The formalization does not import the cited theorem.  Its rigidity chain
(\ghissue{101}--\ghissue{104}) works on the projective dilation of an
arbitrary strategy and derives, from the value alone, the approximate
anticommutation of each player's own $\Variable_1$ and $\Variable_5$
reflections on the dilated state
(\leanid{MIPStarRE.QPBT.MagicSquareRigidity.msVarObsA\_anticommute} and its
counterpart for the second player), together with exact swap isometries built
from those reflections and transfer theorems that pull every post-isometry

\section{Sufficiency for the source's application}
\label{sec:sufficiency}

The only use of the theorem is in the analysis of the Pauli basis test.%
\footnote{\path{references/qpbt-paper/14_analysis_of_the_pauli_basis_test.tex},
lines~152--155 for the symmetric strategy $\strategy = (\ket\psi, M)$ of the
test, lines~197--266 for Lemma~\path{lem:qld-win-implications}, and
lines~326--350 for the invocation.}
There, for each anticommuting tuple $\omega$, the measurements of the test
strategy for the Magic Square questions labelled by $\omega$ form a strategy
$\strategy_\omega = (\ket\psi, M^{\cdot,\omega})$ for $\game^{\MS}$ of value
$\Lambda_\omega = 1 - \eps_\omega$ with $\E_\omega \eps_\omega = O(\eps)$
(item~6 of the lemma), and the theorem is invoked for each $\omega$ to obtain
$\Id \ot M^{\Variable_1,\omega} M^{\Variable_5,\omega}
\approx_{\sqrt{\eps_\omega}} -\Id \ot M^{\Variable_5,\omega} M^{\Variable_1,\omega}$,
the anticommutation consequence for the second player, which is then
averaged over $\omega$ with Jensen's inequality.  The corrected theorem needs
in addition the defects $\Delta_1(\strategy_\omega), \Delta_5(\strategy_\omega)$.
They are available from the lemma: item~7 states that, on average over
anticommuting $\omega$, the first player's trace-postprocessed point
measurement $\{P_a\}$ agrees with the second player's $\Variable_1$ measurement,
$P_a \ot \Id \simeq_\eps \Id \ot M^{\Variable_1,\omega}_a$, and the lemma's
final clause gives the same with the tensor factors interchanged; item~1
states that the point measurements are consistent between the players,
$P_a \ot \Id \simeq_\eps \Id \ot P_a$.  Two applications of the triangle
inequality, after converting $\simeq_\eps$ to $\approx_\eps$ by the lemma's own
final clause, give
$M^{\Variable_1,\omega}_a \ot \Id \approx_\eps \Id \ot M^{\Variable_1,\omega}_a$
on average over $\omega$, that is, $\E_\omega \Delta_1(\strategy_\omega)
= O(\eps)$, and likewise for $\Variable_5$ with the $Z$-basis point
measurement.  Applying the corrected theorem with $\delta_\omega =
\Delta_1(\strategy_\omega) + \Delta_5(\strategy_\omega)$ gives the
anticommutation with error $\sqrt{\eps_\omega} + \sqrt{\delta_\omega}$, and
Jensen's inequality bounds its average by
$\sqrt{\E\eps_\omega} + \sqrt{\E\delta_\omega} = O(\sqrt\eps)$, which is the
bound the source obtains.  The application thus goes through with one extra
triangle inequality and no new input.

\section{Conclusion}

The verdict is a theorem-level gap: the rigidity theorem for the Magic Square
game is false as printed.  Its conclusion forces the two players' $\Variable_1$
and $\Variable_5$ measurements to agree on the shared state, while the
role-symmetric game never sends the same question to both players, and a
perfect strategy that answers the two orientations on two independent
maximally entangled copies violates the conclusion at $\eps = 0$; so does its
symmetric player-role symmetrization, so symmetry alone does not repair the
statement.  The adopted correction assumes that agreement, measured by the
defect \eqref{eq:defect} at the two distinguished variables, and replaces
$\sqrt\eps$ by $\sqrt\eps + \sqrt\delta$; symmetric consistent strategies are
its case $\delta = 0$, both counterexamples have defect $1$, the source's
application supplies the defect up to $O(\eps)$ from its own consistency
subtests, and the cited one-way self-test proves the corrected statement.
The blueprint and \leanid{MIPStarRE.QPBT.exists\_ms\_rigidity} now carry this
form; its proof is the assembly tracked by \ghissue{105}.
