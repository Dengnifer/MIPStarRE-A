\section{Key theorem forms}
Let $K$ be a finite extension of $\mathbb F_p$, let
$\chi(x)=\exp(2\pi i\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb F_p}(x)/p)$, and let
$a,a',b\in K^n$.  The relevant identities are
\[
 \tau^W(a)\tau^W(a')=\tau^W(a+a'),\qquad
 \tau^W(a)^p=I,
\]
and
\[
 \tau^X(a)\tau^Z(b)=\chi(-a\mathbin{\cdot}b)
 \tau^Z(b)\tau^X(a).
\]
The explicit shift and phase matrices are unitary. The second identity implies
that every eigenvalue $\lambda$ of $\tau^W(a)$ satisfies $\lambda^p=1$. If
$V\leq K^n$ and $v\notin V^\perp$, Fourier cancellation takes the form
\[
 \mathbb E_{u\in V}\chi(u\mathbin{\cdot}v)=0.
\]

\section{Source and formal scope}
The source defines the shift and phase matrices and states their product,
$p$-th-power, unitarity, spectral, and twisted-commutation properties in
\cite[lines 1056--1095]{Ji2020MIPStar}. It uses the same character for Fourier
cancellation over a subspace at lines 1124--1132 and extends the twisted
relation to several qudits at lines 1141--1150. These assertions depend on the
characteristic $p$; they are not intrinsically binary.

The formal definitions \path{primeTauShift}, \path{primeTauPhase}, and
\path{primeTauObservable} use the canonical character \path{ffChar} in
characteristic $p$.  Their theorem statements cover the identities above for
arbitrary $p$.  The declarations whose names begin with
\path{tauObservable} or use \path{phaseSign} instead specialize to
$p=2$, where $\chi(x)=(-1)^{\operatorname{Tr}(x)}$.  This specialization is
appropriate only for the subsequent test, whose admissible sizes satisfy
$q=2^k$; it is not substituted for the general statements.

The proofs can be obtained directly from the displayed matrices.  A shift is a
permutation matrix, a phase matrix has diagonal entries of modulus one, and a
tensor product of such matrices is unitary.  Iterating the product identity
gives $\tau^W(a)^p=I$.  Applying this equality to a nonzero eigenvector gives
$(\lambda^p-1)v=0$, hence $\lambda^p=1$.  Finally, when
$v\notin V^\perp$, the map $u\mapsto\chi(u\cdot v)$ is a nontrivial character
of the finite additive group $V$, so its average vanishes.

\section{Blueprint and formalization}
The source-general definitions and results are linked from
\path{blueprint/src/chapter/ch11_qpbt_algebra.tex}, lines~553--633 and
649--660. Their Lean declarations are \leanid{primeTauShift},
\leanid{primeTauPhase}, \leanid{primeTauObservable}, and the accompanying

They quantify an arbitrary prime characteristic and use the corresponding
additive character.

The separately named binary declarations are used by the later QPBT chapters.
They specialize the same formulas to characteristic two and are not linked as
implementations of the unrestricted source nodes. Thus the blueprint, the
general Lean interface, and the paper agree; the binary layer is a consumer
specialization rather than a restriction of the source statement.

\section{Conclusion}
There is no mathematical restriction in the source-facing formal statements:
they retain arbitrary prime characteristic and the corresponding canonical
character.  The characteristic-two declarations are separate corollaries for
the QPBT parameter regime.  The remaining work is to prove the general
identities from the explicit matrices and character orthogonality; it does not
require changing the binary operator definitions.
