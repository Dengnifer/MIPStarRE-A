\section{At a glance}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Verdict: false also on the nonempty source domain.}
  The finite game constructed below has value one and no optimal
  finite-dimensional strategy. Its mathematical proof uses
  \cite[Theorem~1.1]{Slofstra2017Nonclosure}, with an explicit probability
  law, decision predicate, and symmetry conversion. It is not a Lean
  refutation. The intermediate discrepancy is terminal as a
  \emph{documented deviation}; this does not certify the printed assertion or
  the affected entanglement bounds.
  \item \textbf{Difficulty and estimated weight:} the value-preserving and
  positive-slack forms are already proved. The new finite bookkeeping is
  short; a kernel proof of nonattainment would require substantial independent
  work on group representations, followed by the game transfer. The earlier
  estimate of 100--300 proof lines concerned only symmetrization and does not
  estimate this missing work.
  \item \textbf{Mathlib/project split:} existing inner-product, tensor,
  finite-sum and supremum APIs support the elementary transfers. The missing
  group-theoretic theorem and its connection with quantum strategies are
  external mathematical inputs, not existing project or Mathlib lemmas.
  \item \textbf{Key mathematical inputs:} the completed projective form of
  Naimark dilation, \cite[Theorem~5.1]{Ji2020LowIndividualDegree}, and the
  definitions of symmetric games and tensor product values in the source
  \cite[Section~5]{Ji2020MIPStar}.  The note is audited against the
  proof-gap protocol in \ghissue{173}.
  \item \textbf{Formal domain obstruction:} the current Lean game structure
  also permits an empty answer alphabet.  Its strategy type is then empty,
  while the real conditional supremum defining its value is zero.  This gives

  see the domain calculation below and \ghissue{524}.  It does not settle
  attainment for games with nonempty answer alphabets.
\end{itemize}

\section{Key theorem forms}

Three statements must be kept apart. Except in the explicitly empty-answer
example, $\game$ denotes a symmetric two-player one-round game with nonempty
finite alphabets, and $\eps \ge 0$ is real. The notation is introduced below.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Source form (attainment form).}  If
  $\val^*(\game) = 1-\eps$, then there exists a symmetric and projective
  strategy $\strategy$ with $\val^*(\game, \strategy) \ge 1-\eps$.
  \item \textbf{Approximate form (what the source proof establishes).}  If
  $\val^*(\game) = 1-\eps$, then for every $\eps' > \eps$ there exists a
  symmetric and projective strategy $\strategy$ with
  $\val^*(\game, \strategy) \ge 1-\eps'$.
  \item \textbf{Given-strategy form (value-preserving symmetrization).}  If
  $\strategy'$ is any strategy for $\game$ with
  $\val^*(\game, \strategy') \ge 1-\eps$, then there exists a symmetric and
  projective strategy $\strategy$ with
  $\val^*(\game, \strategy) = \val^*(\game, \strategy') \ge 1-\eps$.
\end{enumerate}

The source proof consists exactly of a proof of the third form, applied to a
strategy supplied by the definition of the supremum; this yields the second
form. The first form asserts strictly more than the second, and the passage
from the second to the first is the gap discussed in this note. Both direct
source uses need only the second or third form for their value conclusions.
The entanglement conclusion of answer reduction and its use in compression
remain unresolved, as discussed under ``Uses of the lemma''.

\section{The source assertion}

A two-player one-round game
$\game = (\mathcal X, \mathcal A, \mu, D)$ in compact symmetric notation
consists of a finite question alphabet $\mathcal X$, a finite answer alphabet
$\mathcal A$, a symmetric question distribution $\mu$ on
$\mathcal X \times \mathcal X$, and a decision predicate $D$ treating the two
players symmetrically.  A tensor product strategy
$\strategy = (\ket\psi, A, B)$ consists of a unit vector $\ket\psi$ in a
tensor product $\mathcal H_A \ot \mathcal H_B$ of \emph{finite-dimensional}
Hilbert spaces together with POVMs $\{A^x_a\}$ and $\{B^x_a\}$ for the two
players; its value $\val^*(\game, \strategy)$ is the acceptance probability,
and the value of the game is the supremum
\begin{equation*}
  \val^*(\game) = \sup_{\strategy} \val^*(\game, \strategy)
\end{equation*}
over all tensor product strategies.  A strategy is projective if all its
measurements are projective, and symmetric if the state is
permutation-invariant on $\mathcal H \ot \mathcal H$ and both players use the
same measurements.

The symmetrization lemma of \cite[Section~5]{Ji2020MIPStar} asserts:%
\footnote{The statement is at
\path{references/qpbt-paper/06_nonlocal_games_and_mipstar.tex}, lines~94--99,
and its proof at lines~101--132. The printed statement is retained in
\path{rem:symmetric-strat-limit}; \path{lem:symmetric-strat} states the proved
approximate form in
\path{blueprint/src/chapter/ch12_qpbt_games.tex}.}
if $\game$ is a symmetric game with $\val^*(\game) = 1-\eps$ for some
$\eps \ge 0$, then
\begin{equation*}
  \exists\, \strategy = (\ket{\psi}, M) \text{ symmetric and projective}
  \quad\text{with}\quad
  \val^*(\game, \strategy) \ge 1-\eps.
  \tag{\path{lem:symmetric-strat}}
\end{equation*}

Its proof runs as follows.  For every $\eps' > \eps$ the definition of the
supremum supplies a strategy $\strategy' = (\ket{\psi'}, A, B)$ with
$\val^*(\game, \strategy') \ge 1-\eps'$.  By Naimark dilation in the completed
projective form of \cite[Theorem~5.1]{Ji2020LowIndividualDegree} one may take
$\ket{\psi'} \in \C^d_{A'} \ot \C^d_{B'}$ for some integer $d$ with all
measurements projective.  One then forms the permutation-invariant state
\begin{equation*}
  \ket{\psi} = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt 2}
  \bigl( \ket{0}_A \ket{1}_B \ket{\psi'}_{A'B'}
       + \ket{1}_A \ket{0}_B \ket{\psi'_\tau}_{A'B'} \bigr)
\end{equation*}
on $(\C^2 \ot \C^d) \ot (\C^2 \ot \C^d)$, where $\ket{\psi'_\tau}$ permutes
the two players' registers, and the block-diagonal projective measurements
$M^x_a = \ket{0}\!\bra{0} \ot A^x_a + \ket{1}\!\bra{1} \ot B^x_a$.  Symmetry
of $D$ gives $\val^*(\game, (\ket\psi, M)) = \val^*(\game, \strategy')$.  The
construction is therefore \emph{value-preserving}: applied to any strategy it
produces a symmetric projective strategy of exactly the same value.  What the
proof establishes is thus the given-strategy form, hence the approximate form;
the printed conclusion is the attainment form.

\section{Why attainment does not follow}

The supremum defining $\val^*(\game)$ ranges over strategies on
finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces of unbounded dimension.  The strategies
$\strategy'_{\eps'}$ produced for $\eps' \downarrow \eps$ may require
dimensions $d(\eps')$ growing without bound, and there is no compactness
across dimensions from which a limiting finite-dimensional strategy of value
at least $1-\eps$ could be extracted.  A symmetric projective strategy of
value at least $1-\eps = \val^*(\game)$ would attain the supremum, so the
printed conclusion is equivalent, under its hypothesis, to the assertion that
$\val^*(\game)$ is attained by some strategy.  No general principle supplies
this: it is known, and recalled in the introduction of the source itself, that
the set of finite-dimensional quantum correlations is not closed and differs
from the set of infinite-dimensional spatial correlations, so limits of
finite-dimensional strategies need not be realized by any finite-dimensional
strategy. Non-closure alone would not prove that a game predicate exposes an
unattained value. The construction below supplies such a predicate and settles
that additional question mathematically, using a verified external theorem.

\section{A finite nonempty counterexample}

We use the particular binary linear system constructed in
\cite[Proposition~5.1]{Slofstra2017Nonclosure}, not the inference that an
arbitrary non-closed correlation set has an exposed missing point. Here and
below citations to that article refer to arXiv version~2. The following
finite recipe specifies its matrix $A$ and right-hand side $b$ without an
existential choice of a game.

Start with the group
\[
\begin{split}
 K=\langle x,y,a,b\mid &a^2=b^2=1,\ [a,b]=1,\\
 &yay^{-1}=a,\ yby^{-1}=ab,\ xyx^{-1}=y^2\rangle.
\end{split}
\]
Introduce $c=ab$, and apply the construction of Proposition~4.8 first to
$y=zw$ and then to $x=z_2w_2$. After adjoining the two involutions $t,Z$
as in Proposition~5.1, there are fourteen involutary generators
\[
 a,b,c,z,w,d,e,W,z_2,w_2,r,s,t,Z.
\]
Their linear relations are $abc=1$ and $aZ=J$, where $J$ is the distinguished
central involution. Their ten conjugacy relations $uvu=w'$ are specified by
the ordered triples
\[
\begin{split}
 &(w,a,d),(z,d,a),(w,b,e),(z,e,c),(w,z,W),\\
 &(w_2,w,r),(z_2,r,W),(w_2,z,s),(z_2,s,z),(t,a,Z).
\end{split}
\]
For example, the first elimination replaces $xwx^{-1}=wzw$ by
$wzw=W$ and $xwx^{-1}=W$, and also imposes $xzx^{-1}=z$.
The second elimination gives the four triples involving $z_2,w_2,r,s$.

Apply Lemma~4.4 to this presentation. For each old generator $u$ add
$y_u,z_u,w_u$, and add one generator $f$. Retain the two linear relations,
add $u y_u z_u=1$ and $u f w_u=1$, and for each old triple $(u,v,w')$
add a fresh $g$ with $g y_v z_{w'}=1$. The new conjugacy triples are
$(f,y_u,z_u)$ and $(w_u,y_v,z_{w'})$. This gives 67 generators,
40 linear relations, and 24 conjugacy triples. For each of these last
triples $(u,v,w')$, add seven fresh variables $h_1,\ldots,h_7$ and replace
the conjugacy by the six binary equations
\[
\begin{aligned}
 u+h_1+h_2&=0,&v+h_2+h_3&=0,&h_3+h_4+h_5&=0,\\
 u+h_5+h_6&=0,&w'+h_6+h_7&=0,&h_1+h_4+h_7&=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
These are equation~(4.1) of the cited article. The resulting system
$Aq=b$ has 184 equations and 235 variables. Every row has three variables
except $a+Z=1$, which has two. Column order is the displayed seed order,
then the added variables in the order just specified; within a row use
increasing column order. An executable enumeration of every entry is supplied
with the audit. Its finite checks do not prove quantum nonattainment.%
\footnote{\path{audits/2026-09-22_issue-703-slofstra-system.py};
the \texttt{--json} option prints all column names, row supports and parity bits.
The accompanying mathematical and validation record is
\path{audits/2026-09-22_issue-703-nonempty-attainment.md}.}

Let $V_i$ be row $i$'s ordered support, of size $k_i\in\{2,3\}$.
Define a game $H$ with questions $i\in[184]$ and $j\in[235]$, answers
$\alpha\in\{0,1\}^3$ and $\beta\in\{0,1\}$, and the uniform law
$\mu(i,j)=1/(184\cdot235)$. Its Boolean predicate accepts precisely when
\[
 \sum_{\ell=1}^{k_i}\alpha_\ell=b_i\pmod 2,\qquad
 \alpha_\ell=0\ (\ell>k_i),\qquad
 j\notin V_i\ \text{or}\ \alpha_{\operatorname{pos}_{V_i}(j)}=\beta.
\]
Thus even questions about a variable outside the row enforce a valid Alice
answer. Padding by zero identifies the question-dependent satisfying
assignments in Slofstra's Section~3 with a subset of the fixed eight-answer
alphabet. Each row has a satisfying assignment. A perfect strategy for $H$
has zero marginal probability on every invalid answer, so mapping those
answers to any fixed satisfying assignment recovers a perfect strategy for
the original linear-system game. Conversely its strategies extend by zero
effects on invalid answers.

For this system, Proposition~5.1 proves that $J$ is trivial in every
finite-dimensional representation but nontrivial in approximate
representations. Theorem~3.2 rules out a perfect finite-dimensional strategy;
Proposition~3.4 supplies finite-dimensional strategies approaching perfect
play on every question pair. These imply $\val^*(H)=1$ without attainment:
the acceptance functional
\[
 L(p)=\frac{1}{184\cdot235}\sum_{i,j,\alpha,\beta}
       D_H(i,j,\alpha,\beta)p(\alpha,\beta\mid i,j)
\]
is continuous, is bounded by one, and equals one exactly for perfect play,
because every question pair has positive weight. This specifies the required
supporting functional; it need not expose a unique correlation. Slofstra
uses projective measurements and pure states. His finite-dimensional
strategies are permitted POVM strategies here; in the other direction,
Naimark dilation converts a hypothetical perfect finite-dimensional POVM
strategy to a projective one without changing its correlations. The exclusion
therefore covers exactly the finite-dimensional meaning used in this project.

Now set $Q=[184]\sqcup[235]$ and $R=\{0,1\}^3\times\{0,1\}$, and define
a symmetric game $\widehat H$ by
\[
 \widehat\mu(\mathsf L i,\mathsf R j)
 =\widehat\mu(\mathsf R j,\mathsf L i)=\frac{1}{86480},
\]
with weight zero on equal-role pairs. On answers $(\alpha,\beta)$ and
$(\alpha',\beta')$, its predicate is $D_H(i,j,\alpha,\beta')$ on
$(\mathsf L i,\mathsf R j)$ and $D_H(i,j,\alpha',\beta)$ on the reversed
pair, and is false on equal-role pairs. There are 419 questions and 16
answers, all nonempty. The law sums to one and both symmetry identities hold.

For any strategy for $\widehat H$, taking answer marginals gives a strategy
for $H$ in each orientation, with the parties interchanged in the second.
Its value is the average of those two values. In particular, a perfect
strategy would give a perfect strategy for $H$. Conversely, from a pure
strategy $\psi=\sum_{i,j}\psi_{ij}\ket i\ket j$ for $H$, use two copies
on the common local space $\mathcal H_A\otimes\mathcal H_B$, with state
\[
 \Phi=\sum_{i,j,k,l}\psi_{ij}\psi_{kl}
                 \ket{i,l}\otimes\ket{k,j}.
\]
This is a unit vector invariant under exchange of the players. For a left
question measure $A$ on the first factor and return a fixed unused bit;
for a right question measure $B$ on the second factor and return a fixed
unused triple. Each orientation has exactly the original correlations.
Projectivity is preserved. Thus $\widehat H$ has symmetric projective
strategies approaching value one, while no finite-dimensional strategy
attains it. Taking $\eps=0$ refutes the printed lemma on its nonempty domain.
This mathematical argument neither changes a source game nor changes the
definition of a strategy.

\section{Extent of a possible correction}

The exact value-preserving assertion is stronger than a single approximation
bound: for every symmetric game, the set of values of all finite-dimensional
strategies equals the set of values of symmetric projective strategies.
One inclusion forgets the extra properties; the other is the given-strategy
construction. On the nonempty domain the suprema are consequently equal,
and every strict lower bound on the value is exceeded by a symmetric
projective strategy. Equivalently, the approximate form holds for all
$\eps'>\eps$; using an intermediate threshold also gives a strict conclusion.
No positive numerical loss is necessary in the given-strategy form.

Nonemptiness alone cannot restore the endpoint, by $\widehat H$. Nor can
requiring $\eps>0$: for any $0<c<1$, add a new question $*$, put mass $1-c$
on $(*,*)$ with unconditional rejection, and give the old pairs $c$ times
their old mass. This symmetric nonempty game has value $c$ and no strategy
of value $c$, so its error is $1-c>0$. At value zero, in contrast, every
strategy attains the value, since values are nonnegative and the strategy
class is nonempty. A single answer also forces attainment. These boundary
cases do not extend to the general finite-alphabet domain.

An assumed optimal input strategy would restore the printed endpoint, but
would assume precisely the missing attainment fact. Fixing a dimension would
change the supremum's domain; allowing infinite dimensions would change the
source semantics. Neither is an admissible repair here. A fixed positive
slack is less precise than arbitrary positive slack, and can always be halved.
Thus the exact preservation of attainable values and the all-positive-slack
consequence describe what the cited construction proves without unnecessary
loss. They remain logically weaker than universal attainment. The terminal
classification \emph{documented deviation} records that difference without
adopting the approximate assertion as the printed theorem. In particular,
usefulness in later value estimates does not prove the endpoint or resolve
the independent rank issue below.

\section{An additional obstruction in the formal domain}

The current Lean structure for a symmetric game assumes finiteness of its
answer alphabet but does not require that alphabet to be nonempty.  Consider
the game with question alphabet $\mathcal X=\{*\}$, answer alphabet
$\mathcal A=\varnothing$, the probability distribution concentrated on
$(*,*)$, and the unique decision predicate on the empty answer domain.
Both symmetry requirements hold.  There is no tensor product strategy for
this game: a unit vector in $\mathcal H_A\ot\mathcal H_B$ forces both local
spaces to be nonzero, whereas completeness of Alice's empty measurement
would give
\[
  0=\sum_{a\in\varnothing} A^*_a=I_{\mathcal H_A},
\]
which is impossible on a nonzero space.

Lean defines the game value using the conditional supremum operation on
$\mathbb R$.  For the empty set this operation is assigned the value zero,
as expressed by Mathlib's \leanid{Real.sSup\_empty}.  This is a convention
outside the nonempty domain on which the usual least-upper-bound property
holds; it is not an attainment principle.  Thus \leanid{Game.value} assigns
the example the value $0=1-\eps$ with $\eps=1\geq0$, but it has no symmetric
projective strategy, since it has no strategy at all.  Consequently the exact
current Lean statement is false on its admitted domain, independently of the
unbounded-dimension issue.  The complete verification and its axiom audit are
recorded in \path{audits/2026-09-12_issue-524-symmetrization-obstruction.md}.%
\footnote{The verification reuses the existing
\leanid{nonempty\_of\_unit\_vector} and
\leanid{measurement\_outcome\_nonempty} lemmas.  Its declarations depend only
on \leanid{propext}, \leanid{Classical.choice}, and \leanid{Quot.sound},
not on the unfinished symmetrization theorem.}

This example concerns a boundary of the formal encoding.  Although the
printed definition of a game lists finite alphabets without an explicit
nonemptiness condition, it does not specify a real value for the supremum
over an empty strategy class.  The example therefore does not establish
nonattainment for the intended class of games with nonempty alphabets.
Making that domain condition explicit would exclude this example but would
leave the source proof's attainment step unproved.  The statement-integrity
review requested here was carried out on 2026-09-19 and is the statement
correction recorded below; the printed Lean statement and its proof-gap marker
were replaced there by an unasserted Lean proposition and this refutation,
proved as \leanid{not\_forall\_printed\-Symmetric\-Projective\-Attainment\-Claim}.

\section{Uses of the lemma}

The source invokes the lemma twice.

First, in the soundness proof for the oracularization transformation, the
argument fixes a strategy $\strategy^{\mathrm{orac}}$ for the oracularized game
``with value $1-\eps$'' and applies the lemma to assume without loss of
generality that it is projective.%
\footnote{\path{references/qpbt-paper/10_oracularization.tex},
lines~317--321.}
Here the input is a given strategy, not the value of the game. Only the
Naimark part of the proof is needed: it preserves every correlation and the
Schmidt rank, since it appends local product ancillas and applies local
isometries. The subsequent argument still uses distinct families $A,B$.
This distinction matters for the entanglement bound in the same theorem:
there is no reason to add the entangled role registers used for symmetry.%
\footnote{The source defines the entanglement requirement by minimum Schmidt
rank at \path{references/qpbt-paper/06_nonlocal_games_and_mipstar.tex},
lines~189--197, and invokes that rank at the end of
\path{references/qpbt-paper/10_oracularization.tex}, lines~401--405.
The product ancillas are part of \path{thm:naimark},
\path{references/qpbt-paper/04_preliminaries.tex}, lines~1007--1033.}

Second, in the soundness proof for answer reduction, the argument assumes the
strict inequality $\val^*(\game) > 1-\eps$ and concludes that there is a
symmetric projective strategy of value greater than $1-\eps$.%
\footnote{\path{references/qpbt-paper/11_answer_reduction.tex},
lines~2322--2332.}
Writing $\val^*(\game) = 1-\eps_0$ with $\eps_0 < \eps$ and applying the
approximate form with $\eps' = (\eps+\eps_0)/2$ produces a symmetric
projective strategy of value at least $1-\eps' > 1-\eps$.  A strict lower
bound on the supremum thus passes through the approximate form; attainment is
not used. This justifies the starting strategy for the five local claims,
\path{claim:ar-1} through \path{claim:ar-5}, and the two subsequent lemmas
\path{lem:ar-ar} and \path{lem:ar-ora}. Their value estimates and the
soundness conclusion need no endpoint strategy.

There is a separate limitation when tracing this argument to the entanglement
conclusion of the answer-reduction theorem. If the original state has Schmidt
rank $r$, the symmetrized state in the source proof has rank $2r$: its two
orthogonal role blocks each have the $r$ original Schmidt coefficients, scaled
by $1/\sqrt2$. The last paragraph of the answer-reduction proof says that its
decoded strategy uses the same state as the symmetric strategy chosen at the
start. That does not give a strategy of rank at most $r$ from an arbitrary
input of rank $r$, as the printed entanglement inequality requires.%
\footnote{\path{references/qpbt-paper/11_answer_reduction.tex},
lines~2110--2115 and 2995--2999.}
Even starting from a supplied strategy, the cited symmetrization alone permits
only a factor-two comparison of ranks. A derivation avoiding that loss,
for example by proving the decoding estimates for the original two
measurement families, is a separate obligation; it is not supplied by the
attainment correction. This note does not claim that the entanglement
inequality is false. It withdraws the earlier blanket assertion of sufficiency
for every conclusion of the source transformations.

The compression argument is an affected entanglement consumer. With the
source's successive verifiers $V_n^{(1)},V_n^{(2)}$ and errors
$\eps_1,\eps_2$, it explicitly concludes
\[
 \operatorname{Ent}(V_n^{(2)},1-\eps_2)
 \geq \operatorname{Ent}(V_n^{(1)},1-\eps_1),
\]
where $\operatorname{Ent}(V,p)$ is the minimum Schmidt rank needed to pass
$V$ with probability at least $p$, or infinity if no such strategy exists.
This is a rank guarantee, not merely a comparison of game values. It depends
on the unresolved entanglement part of
answer reduction, so the compression passage cannot be certified by the
positive-slack argument. Its separate value estimates use no attained
supremum, and its completeness starts from an actual perfect PCC strategy;
neither observation establishes the displayed rank inequality.%
\footnote{\path{references/qpbt-paper/13_gap_preserving_compression.tex},
lines~287--326, specifically the entanglement comparison at line~322.
This corrects finding F1 of the first review of \ghpr{707}.}

One cannot simply require symmetrization itself to preserve rank. Consider
the symmetric one-question game that accepts different binary answers.
The deterministic answers zero and one give a perfect product-state strategy.
In a symmetric pure strategy of Schmidt rank one, the local states are equal
up to phase and the common measurement gives identical independent answer
distributions. Its success probability is $2p(1-p)\leq1/2$. Thus a general
rank-preserving version of the symmetrization construction is false; the
additional argument must concern the specific downstream transformation.

In this project, the soundness statements of the Pauli basis test track
quantify over given strategies assumed to succeed with probability at least
$1-\eps$. The caller inventory finds no invocation of this symmetrization
family in the headline proofs. The blueprint records both proved forms as part
of the games vocabulary. Its only proof-dependency edge from a symmetrization
node is from the given-strategy node to the approximate node; there are no further
descendants. Its other actual references occur in the surrounding Naimark
remark and the four symmetrization nodes themselves. The oracularization,
answer-reduction and compression transformations are not formalized in the
active blueprint or Lean subtree.
The exact caller inventory and focused compilation evidence are in the
accompanying audit. Neither direct source invocation requires attainment, but
this does not certify the separate entanglement conclusion just discussed.

\section{Realignment of the Lean statements (2026-09-18)}

This realignment is published as \ghpr{601}.  Following the disposition
recorded for \ghissue{524}, the formalization keeps
the source-labelled statement as printed and adds the approximate form beside
it as a separate, proved Lean declaration.  Three points fix the record.

First, the printed attainment form remains an \emph{open source gap}.  The
declaration \leanid{exists\_symmetric\_projective\_strategy} keeps the source
shape, keeps its proof hole, and keeps its unfaithfulness marker; the blueprint
node \path{lem:symmetric-strat} is still stated as printed and still carries no
proof-level \texttt{leanok} flag.  Nothing in this realignment proves the
printed statement, and the weaker result is not renamed as it.

Second, the approximate form listed under ``Key theorem forms'' above is now
formalized and proved as
\leanid{exists\_symmetric\_projective\_strategy\_approx}, with the equivalent
slack form \leanid{exists\_symmetric\_projective\_strategy\_of\_\allowbreak lt\_value}
beside it, against a new formalization-only blueprint node
\path{lem:symmetric-strat-approx}.  The Lean proof is the source argument
unchanged: the supremum property of $\val^*(\game)$ supplies a strategy above
the strict lower bound, and the already proved value-preserving construction
\leanid{exists\_symmetric\_projective\_strategy\_of\_strategy} symmetrizes it.
The auxiliary facts added for it --- that every strategy value is at most one,
that the set of strategy values is bounded above, and that a nonempty answer
alphabet admits a strategy --- are formalization-only support for
\path{def:tensor-product-value}.

Third, the nonempty answer alphabet appears as an explicit hypothesis of both
