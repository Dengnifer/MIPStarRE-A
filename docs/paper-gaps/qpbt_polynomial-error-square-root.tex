\section{Key theorem forms}

For a fixed real-valued function $f$, the paper's one-variable shorthand is
\[
  \exists C>0\ \forall x>0,\qquad f(x) \leq Cx^C.
\]
The formal one-parameter convention for $f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ is
\[
  \exists A\geq1\ \exists r>0\ \forall x\geq0,\qquad
  0\leq f(x)\leq Ax^r.
\]
This form includes $f(x)=\sqrt{x}$ by taking $A=1$ and $r=1/2$.
The witnesses are chosen before the input $x$; they cannot vary with it.
In the existential QPBT error theorems the function, and hence its power
bound, is chosen before all field parameters, strategies, and error inputs.
The printed inequality is required only on positive inputs. It does not
prescribe $f(0)$, nor does the polynomial shorthand itself add a lower bound
on $f$. In contrast, the formal convention includes nonnegativity and forces
$f(0)=0$. Thus the comparison includes a domain change as well as the
separation of the constants. Both conventions quantify real-valued functions;
the formal bound imposes no condition at negative arguments. They are not
equivalent predicates on such functions.

\section{The source convention}

The asymptotic conventions of \cite[Section~4, Asymptotics]{Ji2020MIPStar} define
$f(x)=\operatorname{poly}(x)$ using the same constant as prefactor and
exponent.  Later, the Pauli basis analysis explicitly obtains
$\delta_{\mathrm{acom}}(\eps)=\sqrt{\eps}$ and
$\delta_{\mathrm{Line}}(\eps)=\sqrt{\eps}$ at lines~508--520 and 649--676 of
the chapter-14 source mirror.\footnote{The corresponding files are
\path{references/qpbt-paper/04_preliminaries.tex:26--29} and
\path{references/qpbt-paper/14_analysis_of_the_pauli_basis_test.tex}.  The
square-root choices conclude the proofs of \texttt{lem:qld-comm-cons} and
\texttt{lem:qld-comm-line-cons}. The discrepancy is tracked in
\href{https://github.com/Dengnifer/MIPStarRE-A/issues/16}{GitHub issue \#16}
and descends from \localissue{0002}.}

The coupled-constant inequality cannot hold for $f(x)=\sqrt{x}$.  At $x=1$ it
would imply $C\geq1$.  For such a constant,
\[
  \frac{\sqrt{x}}{Cx^C}=C^{-1}x^{1/2-C}
\]
is unbounded as $x$ decreases to zero, contradicting the proposed inequality
on all positive inputs. The same obstruction already occurs on $0<x\leq1$;
it does not depend on the formal convention's value at zero. This refutes
the stated square-root choice under the literal scalar shorthand, not the
operator conclusions with a different error contract.

\section{Comparison with the blueprint and formalization}

The blueprint explicitly uses independent constants in the expanded point
and line lemmas, and explains the distinction in its polynomial error
remark.\footnote{Blueprint \texttt{lem:qld-comm-cons},
\texttt{lem:qld-comm-line-cons}, and

The formal helper separates prefactor and exponent.\footnote{The helper is
\leanid{IsPolyErr} in
\doclink{MIPStarRE/QPBT/Games/ErrorFunctions.lean}.}
This preserves decay by a positive power, but changes the literal scalar
content of any theorem that uses the convention. It is not an equivalence
with the printed definition. Formalization marks on the two blueprint lemmas
refer to their explicitly stated replacement convention, not to the printed
coupled-constant assertion.

The finite-arity source convention is retained as
\leanid{PrintedPolynomialBound} in the same file:
\[
 \exists C>0\ \forall x_1,\ldots,x_k>0,\quad
 f(x_1,\ldots,x_k)\leq C(x_1\cdots x_k)^C.
\]
The one-variable square-root specialization is separately retained as
\leanid{PrintedSquareRootPolynomialClaim}. These are unasserted
\texttt{Prop}-valued definitions. The latter records the scalar choice,
not a substitute for the operator conclusions of the two cited lemmas.
Neither declaration is a theorem or a hypothesis of a proof. In particular,
the mathematical refutation above is not represented by a Lean theorem
negating the retained proposition. The actual square-root witnesses are
proved for the existing independent-constant predicate.\footnote{The witnesses are

Both functions are exactly $\sqrt\eps$. Mathlib identifies this with the

\section{Consumers and boundary values}

The square-root estimates enter the joint-point construction
\texttt{lem:qld-4-10}. The source's proof uses successive roots and
quantum linearity under the same polynomial shorthand. The existing Lean
construction instead combines the field-valued point measurements directly
and proves an error $K\eps^{1/8}$, with one universal $K\geq1$ chosen
before the setting. This is another concrete independent-constant bound.
It does not resolve the distinct normalization and ancillary-space
questions in the quoted linearity argument.\footnote{The construction is
\leanid{exists\_combinedPointsWitness} in
\doclink{MIPStarRE/QPBT/Combining/Points.lean}; its proof route is explained
in \path{docs/paper-gaps/qpbt_combined-points-field-valued.tex}.}

The expanded-line witness is also an input to \texttt{lem:qld-xz-lines}.
The point-error bound is a shared contract: supplied-point versions of the
line and extended-line constructors take a function satisfying
\leanid{IsPolyErr}, whereas the existential constructors first produce
that controlled function. They do not accept an arbitrary uncontrolled
point error. Pasting introduces a second error argument $\rho=md/q$.
The shared two-variable predicate bounds a nonnegative function by
$B(x^s+y^t)$ for all $x,y\geq0$, with $B\geq1$ and $s,t>0$ chosen
before the inputs. The replacement of a product by this sum is a separate
discrepancy, not a consequence of separating prefactor and exponent. This
note does not close the pasting-product row. The resulting line
estimates enter the restricted-line averages, Claims 17-1--17-3, the
established extended-line construction, and the global-pair estimate
\texttt{lem:qld-4-7}. Finally the pulling-apart and extraction estimates
use powers of the global-pair error and absorb $\sqrt\eps$ and $md/q$ into
the explicit robustness function.\footnote{Paper chapter 14,
\texttt{lem:qld-4-10}, \texttt{lem:qld-xz-lines}, \texttt{lem:qld-4-13},
\texttt{lem:qld-4-7}, \texttt{lem:qld-construct-the-paulis},
\texttt{lem:qld-unitary}, and \texttt{thm:pauli-appendix}.
The source/blueprint/Lean consumer table is in
\path{audits/2026-09-22-printed-error-contracts.md}; the two-variable
comparison is in \path{docs/paper-gaps/qpbt_pasting-product-error.tex}.}

The established extended-line estimate is $C m g(\eps,md/q)$ for a
function $g$ with the sum bound just described. It concerns directly
indexed lines and completed answers. Neither the printed
$g(m^2\eps,md/q)$ rate nor the source sampling-law and answer comparisons
follow merely from changing the scalar convention. The global-pair and
extraction estimates instead absorb the established errors into
$a(md)^a(\eps^b+q^{-b}+2^{-bmd})$, with universal constants. Those
existing absorption results support the formal soundness conclusions;
they do not certify the missing intermediate source assertions.

At $\eps=0$ both concrete square-root witnesses vanish. The joint-point
error also vanishes by its independent-constant bound. A two-variable line
error at $(0,\rho)$, however, need not vanish when $\rho>0$; only the joint
origin is forced to zero. The final error
$a(md)^a(\eps^b+q^{-b}+2^{-bmd})$ likewise need not vanish at $\eps=0$ for
fixed finite parameters. No zero-error conclusion is inferred from a
positive-domain shorthand by continuity.

The paper's global notation also covers complexity bounds, and error
uses in introspection and oracularization outside the QPBT track. The two
Lean predicates are not replacements for every occurrence of that notation.
In particular, a sum bound for two small errors cannot be substituted for a
product bound for arbitrarily large size parameters. Those occurrences are
distinguished in the consumer inventory; this audit does not certify a
global change of source notation.

\section{Conclusion}

The single-constant shorthand is incompatible with the paper's explicit
square-root choices. Independent constants give proved local estimates
under a different scalar contract. This intermediate gap is closed as
\texttt{documented-deviation}, with the printed claim visible and
unasserted. The separate pasting, extended-line, sampling-law, and
linearity limitations remain as described above. No theorem statement,
proof, game, or earlier repair budget is changed, and no conclusion about
completion of the whole QPBT track follows from this row.
