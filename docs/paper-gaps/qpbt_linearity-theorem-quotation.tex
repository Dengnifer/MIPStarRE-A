\section{At a glance}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Difficulty.} Medium.  The source quotes a fiberwise consequence
  of a state-dependent quantum linearity theorem, but suppresses an average,
  inserts an unnecessary bound on the error, appends an identity at the origin,
  and leaves implicit a compatibility argument for the auxiliary spaces.  The
  factor of two lost between measurement distance and operator distance is not
  one of these: it is a defect of the provider's own printed statement, treated
  in a separate note.
  \item \textbf{Estimated weight.} The corrected single-family theorem and its
  two distance formulations are proved.  The remaining task is a finite
  common-extension construction that makes the auxiliary space uniform over
  the outer parameters in the source's linearity argument.  The combined-point
  conclusion is now proved independently, by rounding a field-valued sandwich
  POVM on the original expanded spaces; it has no remaining extension obligation.
  \item \textbf{Mathlib/project split.} Finite direct sums, extensions of
  operators, and squared norms are standard linear algebra.  The proved
  Fourier--Naimark stability theorem and the two state-dependent distance
  conventions are project-level results.  The replacement combined-point proof
  uses project-local character orthogonality, measurement contraction, and
  projective rounding, with Mathlib finite sums, inner products, and real powers.
  \item \textbf{Key mathematical inputs.} Theorem~10 of
  \cite{Natarajan2017Linearity}, the operator BLR identity, finite averaging,
  and extension by the identity on orthogonal summands.  The remaining
  common-extension reduction is tracked in \ghissue{19}; it is not an input to
  the completed combined-point construction of \ghissue{395}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Key theorem forms}

Let $H$ be a finite-dimensional Hilbert space, let $\rho$ be an arbitrary
density operator on $H$, and let $u\mapsto O^u$ be binary observables on $H$,
indexed by $\mathbb F_2^t$.  The cited theorem assumes the averaged
correlation estimate
\[
  \mathop{\mathbb E}_{u,v}
  \operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(
    O^u O^v O^{u+v}\rho\right) \geq 1-\delta.
\]
Its Fourier--Naimark argument produces an auxiliary space $H'$, a unit vector
$\mathrm{anc}\in H'$, and binary observables $L^u$ on $H\otimes H'$ satisfying,
with
$\rho'=\rho\otimes|\mathrm{anc}\rangle\langle\mathrm{anc}|$,
\[
  L^uL^v=L^{u+v}
  \quad\text{for every }u,v.
\]
Moreover,
\[
  \mathop{\mathbb E}_{u}
  \operatorname{Tr}\!\left((L^u-O^u\otimes I_{H'})^\dagger
    (L^u-O^u\otimes I_{H'})\,\rho'\right)\leq2\delta.
\]
Equivalently, if $d^{\mathrm{bin}}_{\rho'}$ denotes the distance between the
binary projective measurements associated with the two observables, then
\[
  \mathop{\mathbb E}_{u}
  d^{\mathrm{bin}}_{\rho'}(L^u,O^u\otimes I_{H'})^2\leq\delta.
\]
The provider prints $\delta$ for the first, operator-distance bound.  Its
calculation instead proves the second, measurement-distance bound, which is
one half of the squared operator distance.  The factor-of-two discrepancy and
a counterexample to the printed operator bound are given in
\path{docs/paper-gaps/qpbt_linearity-distance-normalization.tex}, tracked in
\ghissue{124}.
In particular, $L^0=I$: exact linearity gives $(L^0)^2=L^0$, while an
observable is invertible.

The source argument uses a parameterized version.  For each pair $(x,z)$ it applies
the theorem to $(r,s)\mapsto O^{x,z,r,s}$.  If $\delta_{x,z}$ is the mean
squared multiplicative defect on that fiber, the corresponding correlation
error is $\delta_{x,z}/2$.  The corrected theorem therefore gives mean squared
operator distance at most $\delta_{x,z}$, equivalently mean squared binary-
measurement distance at most $\delta_{x,z}/2$.  Since the theorem accepts the
actual nonnegative error on each fiber, no assumption that a fiber error is at
most one, and no good--bad fiber split, is needed.  One must still formulate
the finite common-extension step that makes the auxiliary space uniform over
$(x,z)$.

There is a different way to obtain the combined-point conclusion.  Let
$q=2^t$ and let $\psi$ be the normalized state of a projective strategy
winning the Pauli basis test with probability at least $1-\varepsilon$,
with admissible field and degree parameters.  Its expanded state
$\Psi=\hat\psi$ is $\psi$ on $AB$ tensored with maximally entangled states
on $A'A''$ and $B'B''$, each ancillary register having dimension $q^M$,
where $M=2^m$.  The opposite placement pairs are $AA',BA''$ and
$BB',AB''$, in either direction; their operators commute because their
registers are disjoint.  For operator families indexed by point pairs and
outcomes define
\[
  D_\Psi^2(A,B)=\mathbb E_{x,z}\sum_{a,b\in\mathbb F_q}
    \|(A^{x,z}_{a,b}-B^{x,z}_{a,b})\Psi\|^2.
\]
Write $X^x_{i,a}$ and $Z^z_{i,b}$ for the expanded field-valued point
projectors on a placement $i$.  For each player one can construct projective
measurements $Q^{x,z}$ on its original expanded local space such that, for
every directed pair of opposite placements $i,j$,
\[
  D_\Psi^2(Q_i,Q_j),\quad
  D_\Psi^2(Q_i,X_jZ_j),\quad
  D_\Psi^2(Q_i,Z_jX_j)
  \ \leq K\varepsilon^{1/8}.
\]
Here the products are indexed by $(a,b)$ and $K\geq1$ is universal,
independent of the field, the point dimension, and the Hilbert-space
dimensions.  This is the conclusion of Lemma~\path{lem:qld-4-10}, with an
explicit admissible polynomial error; no common ancillary extension is assumed.

\section{Comparison with the quoted statement}

Lines~711--725 of
\path{references/qpbt-paper/14_analysis_of_the_pauli_basis_test.tex} quote the
linearity theorem.  The displayed hypothesis correctly averages over both
group variables.  The displayed conclusion, however, says that the corrected
observable is close for every $u$, whereas Theorem~10 of the cited work gives
closeness only on average over $u$ \cite{Natarajan2017Linearity}.  The
quotation also inserts $\delta\leq1$, which is absent from the provider theorem
and would unnecessarily obstruct applying it with a fiber's actual defect, and
it appends the assertion at the identity, which follows from exact linearity as
above.  These three are the differences between the quotation and the cited
statement.

The factor of two is not one of them.  The provider's equation~(3) proves the
bound $\delta$ for binary-measurement distance; under the operator-distance
convention displayed above this is the bound $2\delta$, and it is the
provider's own Theorem~10 that prints $\delta$ for the operator distance.  The
quotation states its conclusion in the relation $\approx_\delta$, which asserts
only an $O(\delta)$ bound and therefore absorbs the constant factor instead of
reproducing the erroneous one.  The normalization defect and its counterexample
are the provider's and are treated in
\path{docs/paper-gaps/qpbt_linearity-distance-normalization.tex}.

Lines~825--832 construct the family $O^{x,z,r,s}$ and establish approximate
linearity only after averaging over $x,z,r,r',s,s'$.  They then apply the
single-family theorem separately for every $(x,z)$ and state that sufficiently
many zero ancillas avoid a further extension.  The remaining implicit step is
to make the theorem's possibly fiber-dependent auxiliary spaces uniform.

The finite setting admits a standard repair.  Apply the theorem on each fiber
with its actual defect, choose one finite auxiliary space large enough for all
returned spaces, and embed each returned space so that its unit vector maps to
one fixed vector.  Extend the conjugated observables by the identity on the
orthogonal complement.  The embeddings may depend on the fiber because the
observables do.  Averaging then yields the conclusion used later in the
chapter.  This is an open reduction, not a counterexample to the quoted theorem
or to the source's eventual combined-measurement conclusion.

\section{Direct construction of the combined point measurement}

The proof of Lemma~\path{lem:qld-4-10} in \cite{Ji2020MIPStar}, mirrored at
\path{references/qpbt-paper/14_analysis_of_the_pauli_basis_test.tex:728--881}
first forms a sandwich of binary refinements, rounds it, and then uses quantum
linearity and Fourier inversion to recover field-valued outcomes.  The
linearity application at lines~831--832 assumes sufficient zero ancillas.
The following replacement starts with field-valued outcomes and uses the
same orthonormalization principle without that application.

\paragraph{Dimension-independent Parseval transfer.}
Put $\chi(c)=(-1)^{\operatorname{tr}(c)}$, where
$\operatorname{tr}:\mathbb F_q\to\mathbb F_2$ is the field trace.  At a fixed
point pair and placement, suppress $x,z,i$ and write $U_r,V_s$ for the
expanded $X$ and $Z$ observables.  The Fourier formula for the expanded
point effects, at source lines~384--418, is
$X_a=\mathbb E_r\chi(ar)U_r$ and $Z_b=\mathbb E_s\chi(bs)V_s$.
With $[A,B]=AB-BA$, character orthogonality gives
\[
 \sum_{a,b}\|[X_a,Z_b]\Psi\|^2
 =\sum_{a,b}\left\|\frac{1}{q^2}\sum_{r,s}
       \chi(ar+bs)[U_r,V_s]\Psi\right\|^2
 =\mathbb E_{r,s}\|[U_r,V_s]\Psi\|^2.
\]
Indeed the sum over $(a,b)$ of the product of two character coefficients is
$q^2$ when $(r,s)=(r',s')$ and zero otherwise.  This cancels two of the four
inverse powers of $q$ from the squared Fourier normalization, leaving the
uniform average, not an unnormalized sum.  The expanded observable
commutator is the original twisted commutator tensored with a unitary Pauli
operator.  Appending the normalized EPR pairs preserves its norm.  The
estimate used in Lemma~\path{lem:qld-comm-cons}, source lines~466--505,
therefore bounds the average of either side by $C\sqrt\varepsilon$ for a
universal $C$.\footnote{The identities and the estimate are
\path{sum_norm_place_expPointOp_commutator_sq} and
\path{expPoint_comm} in
\path{MIPStarRE/QPBT/Combining/Points/Commutation.lean}.}

\paragraph{Sandwich consistency.}
For fixed $(x,z)$ and opposite placements $1,2$, put
$R_{i,a,b}=Z_{i,b}X_{i,a}Z_{i,b}$.  Positivity follows by writing each effect
as $(X_{i,a}Z_{i,b})^\dagger(X_{i,a}Z_{i,b})$, and summing over $a$ and then
$b$ gives the identity.  Opposite placements commute exactly.  Define
$D^X_a=X_{1,a}-X_{2,a}$, $D^Z_b=Z_{1,b}-Z_{2,b}$,
$W_b=Z_{1,b}Z_{2,b}$ and $K^i_{a,b}=[X_{i,a},Z_{i,b}]$.
For projective measurements $P,Q$ and a vector $w$, expansion of the square
gives
$\sum_c\operatorname{Re}\langle w,P_cQ_cw\rangle
=\|w\|^2-\frac12\sum_c\|(P_c-Q_c)w\|^2$.
Applying this first to $X_1,X_2$ in $W_b\Psi$ and then to $Z_1,Z_2$ in
$\Psi$ yields the exact consistency-defect identity
\[
  1-\sum_{a,b}\langle\Psi,R_{1,a,b}R_{2,a,b}\Psi\rangle
  =\frac12\sum_b\|D^Z_b\Psi\|^2
   +\frac12\sum_{a,b}\|D^X_aW_b\Psi\|^2.
\]
This is also the off-diagonal outcome mass, since both POVMs are complete.
The decomposition
$D^X_aW_b=W_bD^X_a+Z_{2,b}K^1_{a,b}-Z_{1,b}K^2_{a,b}$,
the inequality $\|u+v-w\|^2\leq3(\|u\|^2+\|v\|^2+\|w\|^2)$, and
projective contraction bound the defect by the nonnegative quantity
\[
 c_{x,z}=\frac12\sum_b\|D^Z_b\Psi\|^2
  +\frac32\left(\sum_a\|D^X_a\Psi\|^2
       +\sum_{a,b}\|K^1_{a,b}\Psi\|^2
       +\sum_{a,b}\|K^2_{a,b}\Psi\|^2\right).
\]
Here $\sum_b W_b^\dagger W_b\leq I$ ensures that the first term in the
decomposition does not incur an outcome-count factor.  Point
self-consistency is $O(\varepsilon)$ and the Parseval estimate is
$O(\sqrt\varepsilon)$, so
$\mathbb E_{x,z}c_{x,z}\leq C_0(\varepsilon+\sqrt\varepsilon)$ for a
universal $C_0\geq1$.\footnote{The exact identity, pointwise inequality,
and average are proved in
\path{MIPStarRE/QPBT/Combining/Points/Consistency.lean} as
\path{sandwich_overlap_identity}, \path{sandwich_defect_pointwise_le}, and
\path{avg_sandwichDefectBound_le}.  The function $c$ is
\path{ProjectiveSetting.sandwichDefectBound}.}

\paragraph{Rounding and averaging.}
The explicit orthonormalization lemma says that two opposite POVMs of
consistency defect at most $c\geq0$ admit a projective measurement on the
first POVM's space with squared distance at most $220c^{1/4}$ from it.
Apply it separately for each $(x,z)$, first with the bipartition
$AA'\mid BA''(B'B'')$ and then with $BA''\mid AA'(B'B'')$.
These are regroupings of the existing registers, not extensions.  Choose
the resulting measurements for each point pair.  Two applications of the
square-root averaging inequality give
$\mathbb E c^{1/4}\leq(\mathbb E c)^{1/4}$, whence on each chosen placement
\[
 D_\Psi^2(Q_i,R_i)
 \leq220\bigl[C_0(\varepsilon+\sqrt\varepsilon)\bigr]^{1/4}.
\]
The crosswise exchange $A'\leftrightarrow B''$, $A''\leftrightarrow B'$
fixes the joint state of the two EPR pairs and the strategy state.  It carries these bounds to
$AB''$ and $BB'$ without assuming symmetry of the strategy.\footnote{The
rounding result is \path{projective_rounding_with_explicit_constant},
blueprint \path{lem:ortho-explicit-constant}.  Its application and averaging
are \path{exists_projective_close_sandwich} and
\path{avgOver_rpow_quarter_le} in
\path{MIPStarRE/QPBT/Combining/Points/Orthonormalization.lean}; the EPR
exchange is in the neighboring \path{Placement.lean}.}

\paragraph{The three conclusions and the error.}
Since $R_i-Z_iX_i=Z_i[X_i,Z_i]$, projective contraction bounds
$D_\Psi^2(R_i,Z_iX_i)$ by $O(\sqrt\varepsilon)$.
Transporting the two factors separately to an opposite placement gives
$D_\Psi^2(Z_iX_i,X_jZ_j)=O(\varepsilon)$: each move uses point
self-consistency and square-summability of the other factor.  The triangle
inequality along these comparisons, with one commutation for the other
product order, proves all three desired distances with a universal multiple
of
$[C_0(\varepsilon+\sqrt\varepsilon)]^{1/4}
 +\sqrt\varepsilon+\varepsilon$.
For $0\leq\varepsilon\leq1$ this is bounded by
$K\varepsilon^{1/8}$.  For $\varepsilon>1$, each relevant family has
$\sum_a A_a^\dagger A_a\leq I$, so its squared distance from another such
family on a unit vector is at most $4$.  Increasing $K$ to at least $4$
proves the same bound for every possible error, including zero.\footnote{The
ordered-product bounds are in
\path{MIPStarRE/QPBT/Combining/Points/Sandwich.lean};
\path{chain_bounds} and the scalar estimates are in
\path{MIPStarRE/QPBT/Combining/Points/Closeness.lean}.  The conclusion is
\path{exists_combinedPointsWitness} in
\path{MIPStarRE/QPBT/Combining/Points.lean}.}

\section{Conclusion}

The quotation changes averaged closeness to pointwise closeness, inserts an
unnecessary upper bound on $\delta$, and appends an assertion at the identity
that is derivable from exact linearity.  It does not reproduce the provider's
factor-of-two normalization defect, since it states its conclusion in a
relation that absorbs constant factors; that defect is the provider's own and
is treated separately.  The single-family theorem is now
proved with mean squared operator error at most $2\delta$, equivalently mean
squared binary-measurement error at most $\delta$.  Its use for the family
indexed by $(x,z)$ still requires an explicit finite common-extension argument.
The combined-point theorem, however, is proved by a different, dimension-independent
argument with error $K\varepsilon^{1/8}$ on the original expanded local spaces.
There is no remaining statement discrepancy or proof obligation for that
construction; the common-extension obligation belongs only to the unused
linearity argument.
