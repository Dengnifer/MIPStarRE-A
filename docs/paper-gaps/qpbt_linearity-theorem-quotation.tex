\section{At a glance}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Difficulty.} Medium.  The source quotes a fiberwise consequence
  of a state-dependent quantum linearity theorem, but suppresses an average,
  inserts an unnecessary bound on the error, appends an identity at the origin,
  and leaves implicit an ancilla assumption on the strategy.  The factor of two
  lost between measurement distance and operator distance is not one of these:
  it is a defect of the provider's own printed statement, treated in a separate
  note.
  \item \textbf{Estimated weight.} Resolved.  The corrected single-family
  theorem and its two distance formulations are proved, and so is its
  common-ancilla form: the ancillary space and vector of the Fourier--Naimark
  argument depend only on the length of the index group.  The remaining
  obligation was not the uniformity of the ancilla but the source's assumption
  that the strategy carries zero-state padding.  That assumption is not part of the formal
  setting, and the combined point measurements of the source's Lemma~4.10 are
  therefore constructed on the original space by a route that needs no
  ancilla: the sandwich of the two expanded point measurements, with outcomes
  in $\F_q^2$ from the start, is approximately self-consistent by the same
  calculation as in the source, and the orthonormalization lemma makes it
  projective.  The only new ingredient is a Parseval identity transferring
  the approximate commutation of the binary refinements to the
  $\F_q$-valued measurements without loss.
  \item \textbf{Mathlib/project split.} Finite Fourier orthogonality on a
  finite field, inner-product expansions of squared norms, and the
  submatrix/Kronecker calculus of positive semidefinite matrices are standard.
  The Fourier--Naimark stability theorem, the orthonormalization lemma with
  explicit constant, the expanded-state placement bookkeeping, and the
  approximate commutation of the expanded point projections are project-level
  results.
  \item \textbf{Key mathematical inputs.} Theorem~10 of
  \cite{Natarajan2017Linearity}, the operator BLR identity, Parseval's identity
  for the additive characters of $\F_q$, the orthonormalization lemma of
  \cite{Ji2020MIPStar} in its explicit-constant form, Jensen's inequality for
  the concave function $x \mapsto x^{1/4}$, and the invariance of the expanded
  state under the crosswise exchange of its two EPR pairs.  The discrepancy
  record was opened in \ghissue{19}; the resolution below is carried out in
  \ghissue{117}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Key theorem forms}

Let $H$ be a finite-dimensional Hilbert space, let $\rho$ be an arbitrary
density operator on $H$, and let $u\mapsto O^u$ be binary observables on $H$,
indexed by $\F_2^t$.  The cited theorem assumes the averaged
correlation estimate
\[
  \E_{u,v}
  \operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(
    O^u O^v O^{u+v}\rho\right) \geq 1-\delta.
\]
Its Fourier--Naimark argument produces an auxiliary space $H'$, a unit vector
$\mathrm{anc}\in H'$, and binary observables $L^u$ on $H\otimes H'$ satisfying,
with
$\rho'=\rho\otimes|\mathrm{anc}\rangle\langle\mathrm{anc}|$,
\[
  L^uL^v=L^{u+v}
  \quad\text{for every }u,v.
\]
Moreover,
\[
  \E_{u}
  \operatorname{Tr}\!\left((L^u-O^u\otimes I_{H'})^\dagger
    (L^u-O^u\otimes I_{H'})\,\rho'\right)\leq2\delta.
\]
Equivalently, if $d^{\mathrm{bin}}_{\rho'}$ denotes the distance between the
binary projective measurements associated with the two observables, then
\[
  \E_{u}
  d^{\mathrm{bin}}_{\rho'}(L^u,O^u\otimes I_{H'})^2\leq\delta.
\]
The provider prints $\delta$ for the first, operator-distance bound.  Its
calculation instead proves the second, measurement-distance bound, which is
one half of the squared operator distance.  The factor-of-two discrepancy and
a counterexample to the printed operator bound are given in
\path{docs/paper-gaps/qpbt_linearity-distance-normalization.tex}, tracked in
\ghissue{124}.
In particular, $L^0=I$: exact linearity gives $(L^0)^2=L^0$, while an
observable is invertible.

\paragraph{Common-ancilla form.}
The auxiliary space and vector do not depend on $H$, $\rho$, $\delta$, or the
family $O$.  For every $t\geq1$ there are a Hilbert space $H'_t$ of dimension
$2^t+1$ and a unit vector $\mathrm{anc}_t\in H'_t$ such that, for every
finite-dimensional $H$, every density operator $\rho$ on $H$, every
$\delta\geq0$, and every family of binary observables $O^u$ on $H$ satisfying
the correlation estimate, there are exactly linear binary observables $L^u$ on
$H\otimes H'_t$ with the two conclusions displayed above for
$\rho'=\rho\otimes|\mathrm{anc}_t\rangle\langle\mathrm{anc}_t|$.  Indeed,
in the proof the space $H'_t$ has an orthonormal basis indexed by
$\F_2^t\sqcup\{\bot\}$, the vector $\mathrm{anc}_t$ is the basis vector of
$\bot$, and the projective measurement $\{C^v\}$ dilating the Fourier-square
POVM $B^v=(\widehat{O}^v)^2$ is realized on $H\otimes H'_t$ by the
one-measurement Naimark construction, whatever $H$, $\rho$, and $O$ are.

\paragraph{The statement proved for the combined point measurements.}
Let $\hat{M}^{(\mathrm{Point},X),x}_a$ and $\hat{M}^{(\mathrm{Point},Z),z}_b$,
$a,b\in\F_q$, be the expanded point measurements, projective measurements on
$\mathcal{H}\otimes(\C^q)^{\otimes M}$.  Write $\delta_{\mathrm{Anticom}}(\eps)$ for
the approximate-commutation error of their binary refinements and $\eps$ for
their self-consistency error, both on average over the points and with respect
to the expanded state $|\hat\psi\rangle$.  Then, for every pair $(x,z)$, the
sandwich
\[
  R^{x,z}_{a,b}
  \,=\,\hat{M}^{(\mathrm{Point},Z),z}_b\,
  \hat{M}^{(\mathrm{Point},X),x}_a\,
  \hat{M}^{(\mathrm{Point},Z),z}_b ,
  \qquad a,b\in\F_q ,
\]
is a POVM on the same space, and there is a universal constant $C$ such that,
on average over uniformly random $x,z\in\F_q^m$ and for either bipartition
scheme of the six registers,
\[
  \sum_{a,b}\bigl\|\bigl(R^{x,z}_{a,b}
    -\hat{M}^{(\mathrm{Point},Z),z}_b\hat{M}^{(\mathrm{Point},X),x}_a\bigr)
    |\hat\psi\rangle\bigr\|^2
  \leq C\,\delta_{\mathrm{Anticom}} ,
  \qquad
  \sum_{(a,b)\neq(a',b')}
  \langle\hat\psi|\,(R^{x,z}_{a,b})_{\mathsf{A}\mathsf{A}'}\,
  (R^{x,z}_{a',b'})_{\mathsf{B}\mathsf{A}''}\,|\hat\psi\rangle
  \leq C\,(\eps+\delta_{\mathrm{Anticom}}) .
\]
Applying the orthonormalization lemma to $R^{x,z}$ for each fixed $(x,z)$
yields projective measurements $\hat{Q}^{x,z}_{a,b}$ on
$\mathcal{H}\otimes(\C^q)^{\otimes M}$ with all the conclusions of the source's
Lemma~4.10 and error $\delta_Q(\eps)=O(\eps^{1/8})$.  No auxiliary space, no
linearity theorem, and no Fourier averaging over binary refinements enter.

\section{Comparison with the quoted statement}

Lines~711--725 of
\path{references/qpbt-paper/14_analysis_of_the_pauli_basis_test.tex} quote the
linearity theorem.  The displayed hypothesis correctly averages over both
group variables.  The displayed conclusion, however, says that the corrected
observable is close for every $u$, whereas Theorem~10 of the cited work gives
closeness only on average over $u$ \cite{Natarajan2017Linearity}.  The
quotation also inserts $\delta\leq1$, which is absent from the provider theorem
and would unnecessarily obstruct applying it with a fiber's actual defect, and
it appends the assertion at the identity, which follows from exact linearity as
above.  These three are the differences between the quotation and the cited
statement.

The factor of two is not one of them.  The provider's equation~(3) proves the
bound $\delta$ for binary-measurement distance; under the operator-distance
convention displayed above this is the bound $2\delta$, and it is the
provider's own Theorem~10 that prints $\delta$ for the operator distance.  The
quotation states its conclusion in the relation $\approx_\delta$, which asserts
only an $O(\delta)$ bound and therefore absorbs the constant factor instead of
reproducing the erroneous one.  The normalization defect and its counterexample
are the provider's and are treated in
\path{docs/paper-gaps/qpbt_linearity-distance-normalization.tex}.

Lines~825--832 construct the family $O^{x,z,r,s}$ and establish approximate
linearity only after averaging over $x,z,r,r',s,s'$.  They then apply the
single-family theorem separately for every $(x,z)$ and state that
``by assuming that $|\hat\psi\rangle$ has sufficiently many ancilla zero
qubits, we do not need to extend the state further.''  Two things are implicit
here: that the auxiliary spaces returned for different pairs $(x,z)$ can be
taken to be one and the same, and that the strategy space already contains
that auxiliary space in a zero state.  The next section treats both.

\section{The common-ancilla obligation and the padding assumption}

\paragraph{The ancilla is uniform.}
The first implicit point is settled by the common-ancilla form of the theorem
stated in Section~2: the auxiliary space $H'_t$ and the vector
$\mathrm{anc}_t$ are functions of $t=2\log_2 q$ alone.  Applying the theorem
to the family $(r,s)\mapsto O^{x,z,r,s}$ for each pair $(x,z)$ therefore
produces exactly linear observables $L^{x,z,r,s}$, all acting on the one space
$\mathcal{H}\otimes(\C^q)^{\otimes M}\otimes H'_t$, all compared with the ampliated
originals in the one state
$|\hat\psi\rangle\otimes|\mathrm{anc}_t\rangle$.  Averaging the fiberwise
bounds over $(x,z)$ is then legitimate, and the finite common-extension
construction envisaged in the earlier version of this note (embedding
fiber-dependent spaces into a common one) is unnecessary.

\paragraph{The padding is essential and is not available.}
The second implicit point is a genuine assumption on the strategy, not a
property of the theorem.  Write $H=\mathcal{H}\otimes(\C^q)^{\otimes M}$.  The
exactly linear family lives on $H\otimes H'_t$, and the operator
$\hat{Q}^{x,z}_{a,b}=\E_{r,s}(-1)^{\tr(ra+sb)}L^{x,z,r,s}$ of the source is a
projector on $H\otimes H'_t$.  The source needs it on $H$, and obtains this
by assuming $\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_0\otimes H'_t$ with $|\hat\psi\rangle$ supported on
$|\mathrm{anc}_t\rangle$ in the second factor and every measurement of the
strategy acting as the identity there.  Every strategy is unitarily
equivalent to a padded one, so the assumption costs nothing for the source's
final theorem, which is stated up to local isometries.  It does, however,
change the statement of Lemma~4.10: read on a fixed strategy, the source's
proof establishes the lemma only for padded strategies, which is a
conditional statement of exactly the kind excluded from paper-facing
declarations by \path{docs/paper-gaps/proof-gap-protocol.tex}.

The ancilla cannot be removed afterwards.  Compressing the family along the
ancilla returns the Fourier-square POVM: since $\{C^v\}$ dilates
$\{B^v\}$, one has
$\langle\mathrm{anc}_t|\,\hat{Q}^{x,z}_{a,b}\,|\mathrm{anc}_t\rangle
=\E_{r,s}(-1)^{\tr(ra+sb)}\sum_v(-1)^{v\cdot(r,s)}B^v
=B^{(a,b)}=\bigl(\widehat{O}^{x,z,(a,b)}\bigr)^2$,
where $\widehat{O}^{x,z,(a,b)}=\E_{r,s}(-1)^{\tr(ra+sb)}O^{x,z,r,s}$ is the
Fourier coefficient of the original family.  This is a POVM on $H$, but it is
projective only when the original family is already exactly linear.  Thus no
choice of ancilla and no compression produces the projective measurement the
lemma asserts from the output of the linearity theorem; the projectivity is
purchased precisely by the extra dimensions.

\paragraph{A direct construction on the original space.}
The formal statement of Lemma~4.10 quantifies over every projective strategy
and asks for projective measurements on $H$ for that strategy.  It is proved
as stated, by observing that the passage through binary refinements, exact
linearity, and Fourier averaging can be bypassed.  The source uses the binary
refinements $\hat{M}^{(\mathrm{Point},W),u,r}_b$ because the approximate
commutation of the expanded point projections is established for them
(Lemma~4.9(2) of the source), and the linearity theorem is the device that
returns from $\F_2$-valued to $\F_q$-valued outcomes.  But the commutation
estimate transfers to the $\F_q$-valued measurements exactly.  With
$\chi(a)=(-1)^{\tr(a)}$ and $\hat{W}^r(u)$ the expanded observables, the
definitions give
$\hat{M}^{(\mathrm{Point},X),x}_a=\E_r\chi(ar)\hat{X}^r(x)$ and
$\hat{M}^{(\mathrm{Point},Z),z}_b=\E_s\chi(bs)\hat{Z}^s(z)$, hence
\[
  \bigl[\hat{M}^{(\mathrm{Point},X),x}_a,\hat{M}^{(\mathrm{Point},Z),z}_b\bigr]
  =\E_{r,s}\,\chi(ar)\chi(bs)\,
  \bigl[\hat{X}^r(x),\hat{Z}^s(z)\bigr] .
\]
Parseval's identity for the characters $a\mapsto\chi(ar)$ of $\F_q$, which are
orthogonal, states that for any vectors $v_{r,s}$,
\[
  \sum_{a,b\in\F_q}
  \Bigl\|\E_{r,s}\chi(ar)\chi(bs)\,v_{r,s}\Bigr\|^2
  \,=\,\E_{r,s}\,\|v_{r,s}\|^2 ,
\]
so that, for every placement of the operators on the expanded state,
\[
  \E_{x,z}\sum_{a,b}
  \bigl\|\bigl[\hat{M}^{(\mathrm{Point},X),x}_a,
    \hat{M}^{(\mathrm{Point},Z),z}_b\bigr]|\hat\psi\rangle\bigr\|^2
  =\E_{x,z,r,s}
  \bigl\|\bigl[\hat{X}^r(x),\hat{Z}^s(z)\bigr]|\hat\psi\rangle\bigr\|^2
  =4\,\E_{\omega}\sum_{b,b'}
  \bigl\|\bigl[\hat{M}^{(\mathrm{Point},X),x,r}_b,
    \hat{M}^{(\mathrm{Point},Z),z,s}_{b'}\bigr]|\hat\psi\rangle\bigr\|^2 ,
\]
the last equality because the binary refinements are
$\tfrac12(I\pm\hat{W}^r(u))$.  The right-hand side is
$O(\delta_{\mathrm{Anticom}})$ by the source's Lemma~4.9.  There is no loss
in $q$; an application of Jensen's inequality in place of Parseval would lose
a factor $q^2$ and would not give a polynomial in $\eps$.

Given this, the source's first step goes through verbatim with $\F_q$-valued
outcomes.  The sandwich $R^{x,z}_{a,b}$ of Section~2 is a POVM, and
$R^{x,z}_{a,b}-\hat{M}^Z_b\hat{M}^X_a=\hat{M}^Z_b[\hat{M}^X_a,\hat{M}^Z_b]$
with $\hat{M}^Z_b$ a contraction, so $R^{x,z}$ is
$O(\delta_{\mathrm{Anticom}})$-close to the ordered product.  For the
self-consistency of $R^{x,z}$ between the placements
$\mathsf{A}\mathsf{A}'$ and $\mathsf{B}\mathsf{A}''$ an exact identity replaces
the source's Cauchy--Schwarz chain.  Writing $P_1,P_2$ for the two placements
of an operator $P$, $D^W_c=(\hat{M}^W_c)_1-(\hat{M}^W_c)_2$, and
$w_b=(\hat{M}^Z_b)_1(\hat{M}^Z_b)_2|\hat\psi\rangle$, the placements commute
and the effects are Hermitian projectors, so
\[
  \sum_{(a,b)\neq(a',b')}
  \langle\hat\psi|(R^{x,z}_{a,b})_1(R^{x,z}_{a',b'})_2|\hat\psi\rangle
  \,=\,1-\sum_{a,b}\langle w_b|(\hat{M}^X_a)_1(\hat{M}^X_a)_2|w_b\rangle
  \,=\,\tfrac12\sum_b\|D^Z_b|\hat\psi\rangle\|^2
  +\tfrac12\sum_{a,b}\|D^X_a w_b\|^2 ,
\]
using twice the identity
$\sum_c\langle w|P_cQ_c|w\rangle=\|w\|^2-\tfrac12\sum_c\|(P_c-Q_c)w\|^2$ for
commuting projective measurements $\{P_c\}$ and $\{Q_c\}$.  Expanding
$D^X_a w_b$ as
$(\hat{M}^Z_b)_1(\hat{M}^Z_b)_2D^X_a|\hat\psi\rangle
+(\hat{M}^Z_b)_2([\hat{M}^X_a,\hat{M}^Z_b])_1|\hat\psi\rangle
-(\hat{M}^Z_b)_1([\hat{M}^X_a,\hat{M}^Z_b])_2|\hat\psi\rangle$
and using that the $\hat{M}^Z_b$ are contractions summing to the identity
bounds the consistency defect by
$\tfrac12\sum_b\|D^Z_b\hat\psi\|^2
+\tfrac32\bigl(\sum_a\|D^X_a\hat\psi\|^2+K_1+K_2\bigr)$,
where $K_i$ is the squared commutator sum at placement $i$; on average over
$(x,z)$ this is $O(\eps+\delta_{\mathrm{Anticom}})$.  The defect is manifestly
nonnegative, being a sum of squares.

The orthonormalization lemma (Lemma~4.2 of the source, with explicit constant
$220\,\delta^{1/4}$ in the formalization) applied for each fixed $(x,z)$ to the
POVM $R^{x,z}$ on $\mathsf{A}\mathsf{A}'$ against $R^{x,z}$ on
$\mathsf{B}\mathsf{A}''$ produces a projective measurement
$\hat{Q}^{x,z}_{a,b}$ on $H$ with
$\sum_{a,b}\|((\hat{Q}^{x,z}_{a,b}-R^{x,z}_{a,b})_{\mathsf{A}\mathsf{A}'})
\hat\psi\|^2\leq220\,c_{x,z}^{1/4}$, where $c_{x,z}$ is the bound on the
defect just obtained; since $x\mapsto x^{1/4}$ is concave,
$\E_{x,z}c_{x,z}^{1/4}\leq(\E_{x,z}c_{x,z})^{1/4}$, which is
$O(\eps^{1/8})$.  The measurement for Bob's side is obtained in the same way
from the bipartition $\mathsf{B}\mathsf{A}''\mid\mathsf{A}\mathsf{A}'
\mathsf{B}'\mathsf{B}''$.  The remaining conclusions of Lemma~4.10 are the
triangle inequality along
$\hat{Q}\approx R\approx\hat{M}^Z\hat{M}^X\approx\hat{M}^X\hat{M}^Z$, together
with the consistency of the ordered products across opposite placements,
$(\hat{M}^Z_b\hat{M}^X_a)_1\approx(\hat{M}^X_a\hat{M}^Z_b)_2$, which follows
from the self-consistency of each factor and the contraction property of the
other, exactly as in the source's calculation.  The symmetric equivalents on
$\mathsf{B}\mathsf{B}'$ and $\mathsf{A}\mathsf{B}''$ do not require a symmetric
strategy: the permutation of the six registers that exchanges
$\mathsf{A}'\leftrightarrow\mathsf{B}''$ and
$\mathsf{A}''\leftrightarrow\mathsf{B}'$ fixes $|\hat\psi\rangle$, because
each EPR vector is symmetric, and carries the placement
$\mathsf{A}\mathsf{A}'$ to $\mathsf{A}\mathsf{B}''$ and
$\mathsf{B}\mathsf{A}''$ to $\mathsf{B}\mathsf{B}'$, so every squared distance
on one placement equals the same squared distance on its image.

\section{Conclusion}

The quotation changes averaged closeness to pointwise closeness, inserts an
unnecessary upper bound on $\delta$, and appends an assertion at the identity
that is derivable from exact linearity.  It does not reproduce the provider's
factor-of-two normalization defect, since it states its conclusion in a
relation that absorbs constant factors; that defect is the provider's own and
is treated separately.  The single-family theorem is proved with mean squared
operator error at most $2\delta$, equivalently mean squared binary-measurement
error at most $\delta$, and its ancilla depends only on the index length, so
the fiberwise application is uniform.  What the source additionally assumes,
zero-state padding of the strategy, is not a property of an arbitrary
projective strategy and cannot be dispensed with in the source's route, since
the ancilla is what makes the Fourier-averaged family projective.  The combined
point measurements are therefore constructed on the original space directly:
Parseval's identity transfers the approximate commutation to the
$\F_q$-valued point measurements without loss, the sandwich POVM is then
approximately self-consistent, and the orthonormalization lemma makes it
projective with error $O(\eps^{1/8})$.
