\section{At a glance}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Difficulty.} Medium.  The source quotes a fiberwise consequence
  of a state-dependent quantum linearity theorem, but suppresses two averages,
  loses a factor of two when converting measurement distance to operator
  distance, and leaves implicit a compatibility argument for the auxiliary
  spaces.
  \item \textbf{Estimated weight.} One faithful theorem for a single family,
  followed by a finite common-extension construction that makes the auxiliary space
  uniform over the outer parameters.
  \item \textbf{Mathlib/project split.} Finite direct sums, extensions of
  operators, and squared norms are standard linear algebra.  The imported
  quantum linearity theorem and its state-dependent metric are project-level
  inputs.
  \item \textbf{Key mathematical inputs.} Theorem~10 of
  \cite{Natarajan2017Linearity}, finite averaging, and extension by the identity
  on orthogonal summands.  This reduction is tracked in
  \ghissue{19}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Key theorem forms}

Let $H$ be a finite-dimensional Hilbert space, let $\rho$ be an arbitrary
density operator on $H$, and let $u\mapsto O^u$ be binary observables on $H$,
indexed by $\mathbb F_2^t$.  The cited theorem assumes the averaged
correlation estimate
\[
  \mathop{\mathbb E}_{u,v}
  \operatorname{Tr}\!\left(O^u O^v O^{u+v}\rho\right) \geq 1-\delta.
\]
It produces an auxiliary space $H'$, a unit vector $\mathrm{anc}\in H'$, and
binary observables $L^u$ on $H\otimes H'$ satisfying, with
$\rho'=\rho\otimes|\mathrm{anc}\rangle\langle\mathrm{anc}|$,
\[
  L^uL^v=L^{u+v}
  \quad\text{for every }u,v.
\]
Moreover,
\[
  \mathop{\mathbb E}_{u}
  \operatorname{Tr}\!\left((L^u-O^u\otimes I_{H'})^\dagger
    (L^u-O^u\otimes I_{H'})\,\rho'\right)\leq2\delta.
\]
Equivalently, the mean squared distance between the associated binary
projective measurements is at most $\delta$.  The normalization identity and
a counterexample to the stronger operator bound are given in
\path{docs/paper-gaps/qpbt_linearity-distance-normalization.tex}.
In particular, $L^0=I$: exact linearity gives $(L^0)^2=L^0$, while an
observable is invertible.

The chapter needs a parameterized version.  For each pair $(x,z)$ it applies
the theorem to $(r,s)\mapsto O^{x,z,r,s}$.  Since the provider theorem accepts
the actual nonnegative defect on each fiber, no assumption that a fiber defect
is at most one, and no good--bad fiber split, is needed.  One must still
formulate the finite common-extension step that makes the auxiliary space
uniform over $(x,z)$.

\section{Comparison with the quoted statement}

Lines~711--725 of
\path{references/qpbt-paper/14_analysis_of_the_pauli_basis_test.tex} quote the
linearity theorem.  The displayed hypothesis correctly averages over both
group variables.  The displayed conclusion, however, says that the corrected
observable is close for every $u$, whereas Theorem~10 of the cited work gives
closeness only on average over $u$ \cite{Natarajan2017Linearity}.  Moreover,
the provider's equation~(3) proves the bound $\delta$ for binary-measurement
distance; under the operator-distance convention displayed above this is the
bound $2\delta$.  Identifying it with the same bound $\delta$ loses a factor of
two.  The quotation also inserts $\delta\leq1$, which is absent from the
provider theorem and would unnecessarily obstruct applying it with a fiber's
actual defect.  The assertion at the identity follows from exact linearity as
above.

Lines~825--832 construct the family $O^{x,z,r,s}$ and establish approximate
linearity only after averaging over $x,z,r,r',s,s'$.  They then apply the
single-family theorem separately for every $(x,z)$ and state that sufficiently
many zero ancillas avoid a further extension.  The remaining implicit step is
to make the theorem's possibly fiber-dependent auxiliary spaces uniform.

The finite setting admits a standard repair.  Apply the theorem on each fiber
with its actual defect, choose one finite auxiliary space large enough for all
returned spaces, and embed each returned space so that its unit vector maps to
one fixed vector.  Extend the conjugated observables by the identity on the
orthogonal complement.  The embeddings may depend on the fiber because the
observables do.  Averaging then yields the conclusion used later in the
chapter.  This is an open reduction, not a counterexample to the quoted theorem
or to the source's eventual combined-measurement conclusion.

\section{Blueprint and formalization status}

Theorem~\path{thm:linearity} and
Remark~\path{rem:linearity-import} in
\path{blueprint/src/chapter/ch15_qpbt_combining.tex} record respectively the
averaged provider theorem and the common-ancilla obligation for its fiberwise
use.

The declaration \path{exists_exactly_linear_observables} in
\path{MIPStarRE/QPBT/Combining/Linearity.lean} assumes a nonnegative defect, a
positive semidefinite trace-one operator, binary observables, and the averaged
three-observable correlation.  It returns one finite ancillary space and
normalized ancillary vector for that family, exactly linear binary observables
on the extension, and only the averaged squared operator-distance bound.  It
neither assumes $\delta\leq1$ nor asserts pointwise closeness, and it carries
the corrected bound $2\delta$ rather than the printed $\delta$.  It therefore
matches the corrected blueprint theorem, and its proof is complete; so is that
of its measurement-distance companion
\path{exists_exactly_linear_observables_binaryObservableDistSq}, which states
the equivalent bound $\delta$.  Both are linked from
Theorem~\path{thm:linearity}.  The normalization analysis was carried out in
\ghissue{124} and the statement repair in \ghissue{129}; both are closed.
What remains open is the finite common-extension construction for the family
indexed by $(x,z)$, for which no Lean declaration yet exists.

Issue~\#19 introduced this discrepancy record for the extraction skeleton;
\ghissue{47} owns the Stage~4.3 proof work consumed downstream.  The chapter-16
extraction declarations consume the concrete \path{GlobalPairWitness}; they
do not assume a new fiberwise linearity hypothesis or a common ancillary space.

\section{Conclusion}

The quotation strengthens average closeness to pointwise closeness and loses a
factor of two in translating binary-measurement distance to operator distance.
Its use for the family indexed by $(x,z)$ also requires an explicit finite
common-extension argument.  The blueprint records the corrected averaged
bound and the Lean theorem proves it; only the common-extension construction
remains open.
