\section{Key theorem forms}

Let $S$ and $T$ be binary observables and let $\rho$ be a positive
semidefinite operator of trace one.  Write
\begin{align*}
  d_\rho(S,T)^2
    &:= \Re\tr\big((S-T)^\dagger(S-T)\rho\big), \\
  \dbin_\rho(S,T)^2
    &:= \sum_{s\in\{+,-\}}
      \Re\tr\big((P_S^s-P_T^s)^\dagger(P_S^s-P_T^s)\rho\big),
  \qquad P_S^\pm := \frac{\Id\pm S}{2}.
\end{align*}
Then
\begin{align}
  \dbin_\rho(S,T)^2 &= \frac12 d_\rho(S,T)^2,
    \tag{normalization}\label{eq:normalization}\\
  d_\rho(S,T)^2 &= 2-2\Re\tr(ST\rho),
    \tag{operator correlation}\label{eq:operator-correlation}\\
  \dbin_\rho(S,T)^2 &= 1-\Re\tr(ST\rho).
    \tag{binary correlation}\label{eq:binary-correlation}
\end{align}
Consequently, the quantum linearity conclusion can be written either as
\[
  \mathbb E_a\dbin_{\rho'}(\mathcal A(a),A(a))^2\leq\delta
\]
or, equivalently, as
\[
  \mathbb E_a d_{\rho'}(\mathcal A(a),A(a))^2\leq2\delta.
\]
It cannot use the same bound $\delta$ for the squared operator distance.%
\footnote{The adapter declarations are \leanid{binaryObservableDistSq},
\leanid{binaryObservableDistSq\_eq\_stateDepDistSq\_div\_two}, and the two
correlation identities in
\doclink{MIPStarRE/QPBT/Combining/Linearity/BooleanFourier.lean}.}

\section{The source assertion and discrepancy}

Natarajan and Vidick define the distance between POVMs by summing the squared
state-dependent distances between corresponding square roots
\cite[Section~3.1]{Natarajan2017Linearity}.  For binary projective
measurements, their calculation gives
\[
  \dbin_\rho(S,T)^2
    = 1-\frac12\tr_\rho(ST+TS)
    = \frac12\tr_\rho((S-T)^2).
  \tag{source equation (3)}
\]
This is equation~(3) at lines~903--910 of the in-repository mirror.%
\footnote{See \path{references/nv-paper/fullpaper.tex}, lines~866--912; the
quantum linearity theorem and its Fourier--Naimark proof are at
lines~1074--1112.}
The next line identifies the last expression with the squared
state-dependent operator distance.  Under the standard definition displayed
above, it is one half of that distance.  Thus the calculation through
line~910 is correct, but line~911 loses a factor of two.

The Fourier--Naimark proof is consistent with the measurement normalization.
Its consistency deficit is at most $\delta/2$; the squared binary-measurement
distance is twice that deficit, and the squared operator distance is twice the
measurement distance.  It therefore gives the two equivalent corrected forms
in Section~2.

\section{A four-point counterexample}

The bound $\delta$ for the squared operator distance is false.  Let
$G=(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2$, choose $r\in G\setminus\{0\}$, take the
one-dimensional state $\rho=1$, and define scalar binary observables by
\[
  A(a)=\begin{cases}
    -1,&a=r,\\
     1,&a\neq r.
  \end{cases}
\]
For the characters $\chi_u(a)=(-1)^{u\cdot a}$, the normalized Fourier
coefficients form the multiset
$\{\tfrac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac12,-\tfrac12\}$.  Hence
\[
  \mathbb E_{a,b}A(a)A(b)A(a+b)
    =\sum_u\widehat A(u)^3=\frac14,
\]
so the correlation hypothesis holds with $\delta=3/4$.

Every scalar exact Boolean representation is a character, and
\[
  \mathbb E_a |A(a)-\chi_u(a)|^2
    =2-2\widehat A(u)\geq1>\delta.
\]
An ancillary space does not improve this bound.  Exact linearity makes the
corrected observables a commuting family of Hermitian involutions, whose
simultaneous spectral subspaces carry characters of $G$.  Evaluation in any
density operator is therefore a convex combination of the scalar character
correlations above.  The mean squared operator distance remains at least
$1$, whereas the corresponding binary-measurement distance is at least
$1/2$, which is compatible with the corrected theorem.

\section{Correction and blueprint/formalization status}

The QPBT paper quotes the theorem at lines~711--725 of its Pauli-basis

defect as the correlation deficit.  The existing formal theorem still bounds
the average of \leanid{stateDepDistSq} by $\delta$, so its conclusion is too
strong by a factor of two and it is not marked as matching the corrected
blueprint statement.%

\section{Conclusion}

The provider's binary-measurement calculation is correct through the factor
$1/2$ in equation~(3), but its final identification with squared
state-dependent operator distance is incorrect.  The valid conclusion has
mean squared binary-measurement error at most $\delta$, equivalently mean
squared operator error at most $2\delta$.  The four-point example rules out
the stronger operator-distance bound even with arbitrary ancillary extensions.
