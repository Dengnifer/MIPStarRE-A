\section{At a glance}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Difficulty.} Medium.  The operator estimate imported by the
  source is plausible, but its stated two-variable polynomial contract is
  contradicted by a two-dimensional example at every fixed positive input
  consistency error.
  \item \textbf{Estimated weight.} Correcting the scalar contract changes one
  shared predicate.  Formalizing the resulting quantitative pasting estimate
  is expected to require roughly 300--600 lines using the existing distance
  calculus.
  \item \textbf{Mathlib/project split.} The obstruction is elementary real
  analysis and finite-dimensional measurement theory.  The repaired proof is
  primarily project-local consistency and Cauchy--Schwarz calculus.
  \item \textbf{Key mathematical inputs.} A positive real power tends to zero
  at zero, while a fixed positive consistency error need not decrease with the
  collision error.
  The discrepancy is tracked in \ghissue{196}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Key theorem forms}

The source asks for an error function
$\delta_{\mathrm{pasting}}(\eta,\delta)$ satisfying
\[
  \delta_{\mathrm{pasting}}(\eta,\delta)
    = \operatorname{poly}(\eta,\delta)
\]
and bounding the final consistency defect whenever the three input defects are
at most $\delta$ and the codeword-collision probability is at most $\eta$.
Under the paper's global convention, the displayed polynomial assertion means
that there is a universal $C>0$ such that
\[
  0 \leq \delta_{\mathrm{pasting}}(\eta,\delta)
    \leq C(\eta\delta)^C
  \qquad (\eta,\delta>0).
  \tag{product contract}
\]
The imported proof instead accumulates terms depending separately on the two
errors, including $O(\delta^{1/2})$, $O((\delta+\eta)^{1/2})$, and $\eta$.
A compatible corrected theorem form would use, for example, an upper bound by
$A(\eta^r+\delta^s)$ for universal $A\geq1$ and $r,s>0$.

\section{The source assertion}

Lemma~\texttt{lem:pasting} of \cite[lines~504--525]{Ji2020MIPStar} imports
Fact~4.35 from the NEEXP paper.  Its error is declared to be
$\operatorname{poly}(\eta,\delta)$.  The asymptotic convention in
\cite[lines~19--29]{Ji2020MIPStar} requires every variable to occur in the
product on the right side of the product contract.  The proof of the imported
fact, however, bounds the two sources of error independently; see
\cite[lines~1030--1209]{Natarajan2018NEEXP}.%

\section{A two-dimensional counterexample}

Fix $0<\rho<1$.  Let every question set, both codeword sets, and the second
answer set be a singleton, and let the first answer set be $\{0,1\}$.  Use the
point-mass question distribution and local Hilbert spaces $\C^2$.  In the
standard basis, take the normalized bipartite state
\[
  \ket{\psi_\rho}
    = \sqrt{1-\rho}\ket{0,0}+\sqrt{\rho}\ket{1,1}.
\]
Let both codeword measurements have their sole effect equal to the identity,
and evaluate the sole first codeword to $0$.  Let the joint answer measurement
be the standard-basis projective measurement: its effects at $(0,*)$ and
$(1,*)$ are respectively $\ket{0}\!\bra{0}$ and $\ket{1}\!\bra{1}$.

The second codeword set is a singleton, so its pairwise-collision hypothesis is
vacuous for every $\eta\geq0$.  The second marginal is exactly consistent, and
the perfectly correlated state makes the joint answer measurement exactly
self-consistent.  The first marginal differs from the constantly-zero
codeword answer precisely on the $\ket{1,1}$ component, so its consistency
defect is $\rho$.  Consequently all hypotheses of the pasting lemma hold with
$\delta=\rho$.

The pasted measurement also has a single codeword outcome and its effect is the
identity.  After evaluation it is deterministic at $(0,*)$, whereas the joint
answer measurement returns $(1,*)$ with probability $\rho$.  Their final
consistency defect is therefore $\rho$.  Thus the asserted conclusion would
require
\[
  \rho \leq \delta_{\mathrm{pasting}}(\eta,\rho)
  \qquad\text{for every }\eta>0.
\]
The product contract gives the opposite asymptotic bound
$\delta_{\mathrm{pasting}}(\eta,\rho)\leq C(\eta\rho)^C$.  Since $C>0$, the
right side tends to zero with $\eta$, which is a contradiction.  As $\rho$ can
be arbitrarily small, this failure is not confined to the large-error boundary.

\section{Status of the one-sided formal statement}

The corrected scalar contract removes the obstruction above, but the proof of
the paper-facing declaration remains open (\ghissue{201}).  This section
records the precise obstacle.

\subsection*{The two theorem forms}

The source states the lemma for symmetric strategies: the shared state is a
vector in $\mathcal H\ot\mathcal H$ invariant under the permutation of the two
factors, and both players use the same measurement operators
(\cite[lines~84--86 and~174--176]{Ji2020MIPStar}).  Under this convention the
relation $X\ot\mathrm{Id}\simeq_\delta\mathrm{Id}\ot Y$ implies
$Y\ot\mathrm{Id}\simeq_\delta\mathrm{Id}\ot X$, and the imported proof
(\cite[lines~1150--1175]{Natarajan2018NEEXP}) uses this freely: it moves the
fine-grained second codeword effect $(G_2)^x_{g_2}$ between the two factors and
bounds the resulting collision term by $\eta$ using the positivity of
$\langle\psi|\,(G_1G_2G_1)\ot G_2\,|\psi\rangle$.

The formal declaration quantifies over an arbitrary unit vector of the
bipartite space and assumes only the three forward comparisons
\eqref{eq:pasting-1}--\eqref{eq:pasting-2} of the source, in which every
answer operator acts on the first factor and every codeword operator on the
second.  No operator indexed by a codeword $g_2$ acts on the first factor, so
the register move of the source is not available.

\subsection*{A one-sided reduction}

Write $\psi_a=(A_a\ot\mathrm{Id})\psi$ for the post-measurement vectors of the
joint answer measurement, $P_{a_1}=(G_1)^x_{[\operatorname{eval}_{y_1}=a_1]}$,
$G_{g_2}=(G_2)^x_{g_2}$, and $[a_2]$ for the set of second codewords
evaluating to $a_2$ at $y_2$.  Since the first-factor projections are
orthogonal and commute with every second-factor operator, the defect of the
conclusion \eqref{eq:pasting-3} at a fixed question equals
\[
  \sum_a\sum_{g_2\notin[a_2]}
    \bigl\|(\mathrm{Id}\ot G_{g_2})\psi_a\bigr\|^2
  +\sum_a\sum_{g_2\in[a_2]}
    \bigl\langle(\mathrm{Id}\ot G_{g_2})\psi_a\bigm|
      \mathrm{Id}\ot(\mathrm{Id}-P_{a_1})\bigm|
      (\mathrm{Id}\ot G_{g_2})\psi_a\bigr\rangle .
\]
The first sum is the second-marginal defect of \eqref{eq:pasting-1}, at most
$\delta$ on average.  The second sum is at most the \emph{pinched defect}
\[
  \Delta_1=\sum_{a_1}\sum_{g_2}
    \bigl\langle(\mathrm{Id}\ot G_{g_2})\psi_{a_1}\bigm|
      \mathrm{Id}\ot(\mathrm{Id}-P_{a_1})\bigm|
      (\mathrm{Id}\ot G_{g_2})\psi_{a_1}\bigr\rangle,
  \qquad \psi_{a_1}=(A_{a_1}\ot\mathrm{Id})\psi,
\]
which is the defect of the first-marginal comparison against the family
$\sum_{g_2}G_{g_2}P_{a_1}G_{g_2}$.  The hypothesis \eqref{eq:pasting-1} for
$i=1$ bounds the same expression without the pinching by $G_2$.  The theorem
is therefore equivalent, up to an additive $\delta$, to the assertion that
measuring the second codeword family on the second factor does not destroy the
first-codeword mass of the vectors $\psi_{a_1}$.

\subsection*{The obstacle}

In the exact case $\delta=\eta=0$ the pinched defect vanishes one-sidedly:
every fine effect $G_{g_2}$ then coincides with a coarse projector
$Q^{y_2}_{a_2}=(G_2)^x_{[\operatorname{eval}_{y_2}=a_2]}$, the coarse
projector moves to the first factor by \eqref{eq:pasting-1} for $i=2$, and
there it commutes with $\mathrm{Id}\ot P_{a_1}$.  For $\eta>0$ the fine
effects differ from the coarse projectors by the collision cross terms
\[
  \sum_{a}\;\sum_{\substack{g_2\neq g_2'\\ g_2,g_2'\in[a_2]}}
    \bigl\langle\psi\bigm|(A_a\ot\mathrm{Id})
      (\mathrm{Id}\ot G_{g_2}P_{a_1}G_{g_2'})\bigm|\psi\bigr\rangle .
\]
These terms are not positive, and no operator on the first factor is indexed
by the second codeword.  The available one-sided estimates bound each term by
$\|(\mathrm{Id}\ot G_{g_2})\psi\|\,\|(\mathrm{Id}\ot G_{g_2'})\psi\|$, and the
conditional collision bound then yields, after averaging over $y_2$, a bound
of order $\eta$ times the expected number of second codewords colliding with a
given one.  In the intended applications this number is comparable to
$|\mathcal G_2|/|\mathcal R_2|$, so the resulting bound is not polynomial in
$(\eta,\delta)$ with universal constants.  Expressing a fine effect as an
average of coarse projectors over independent samples of $y_2$ carries the
same multiplicity.  The symmetric convention avoids this: the second-factor
codeword effect is replaced by the identical effect on the first factor, the
cross term becomes an expectation of a tensor product of positive operators,
and the collision indicator can be averaged against a total mass at most one.

\subsection*{What is known about the one-sided statement}

No counterexample is known.  The self-consistency hypothesis
\eqref{eq:pasting-2} together with \eqref{eq:pasting-1} for $i=2$ forces the
post-measurement vectors $(\mathrm{Id}\ot G_{g_2})\psi$ to be approximately
fixed by the first-factor projector $A^{x,y_1,y_2}_{[g_2]}$ for every $y_2$,
and hence, using the collision bound, the reduced state of the second player
is nearly diagonal in the eigenbasis of $G_2$ (its off-diagonal part has
squared Frobenius norm at most $4\delta/(1-\eta)$).  This suggests that the
hypotheses force the strategy to behave symmetrically where the codeword
operators act, and that the one-sided statement is true, but a quantitative
argument turning this into a bound on $\Delta_1$ is not in the source and is
not available.

\subsection*{Repair options}

Following the protocol in \path{docs/paper-gaps/proof-gap-protocol.tex}, the
paper-facing declaration keeps the source form with a tracked incomplete
proof.  Two dischargers are possible.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Prove a one-sided bound on the pinched defect $\Delta_1$ from the
  three forward comparisons and the collision bound.  This closes the
  declaration as stated.
  \item Expose the source's ambient convention as a faithful boundary
  hypothesis: assume that the shared vector is invariant under the permutation
  of the two factors, and restate the comparisons with the common operator
  families.  Under this hypothesis the coarse consistency of $G_2$ across the
  factors, the fine consistency (with error $\delta+\eta$ by the collision
  bound), and the register move follow as in the imported proof.
\end{enumerate}
No declaration on the main branch or on any open branch invokes the pasting
theorem, so every consumer accepts either form.  The formalization of the
blueprint proof of Lemma~\texttt{lem:pasting} is deferred until one of the two
dischargers is chosen.

\section{Conclusion}

The literal product-form interpretation of
$\operatorname{poly}(\eta,\delta)$ makes the stated pasting lemma false.  This
is a theorem-level scalar discrepancy, not a missing formal-library lemma.  The
natural repair is to adopt a multivariate polynomial-smallness convention that
allows separate positive powers of the input errors, matching the terms in the
imported proof.  Adding an unadvertised coupling hypothesis between $\eta$ and
$\delta$ would also avoid the counterexample, but would strengthen the source
theorem and is therefore not the preferred repair.
