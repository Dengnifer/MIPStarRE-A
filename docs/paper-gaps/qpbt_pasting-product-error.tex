\section{Key theorem forms}

The source asks for an error function
$\delta_{\mathrm{pasting}}(\eta,\delta)$ satisfying
\[
  \delta_{\mathrm{pasting}}(\eta,\delta)
    = \operatorname{poly}(\eta,\delta)
\]
and bounding the final consistency defect whenever the three input defects are
at most $\delta$ and the codeword-collision probability is at most $\eta$.
Under the paper's global convention, the displayed polynomial assertion means
that there is a universal $C>0$ such that
\[
  0 \leq \delta_{\mathrm{pasting}}(\eta,\delta)
    \leq C(\eta\delta)^C
  \qquad (\eta,\delta>0).
  \tag{product contract}
\]
The imported proof instead accumulates terms depending separately on the two
errors, including $O(\delta^{1/2})$, $O((\delta+\eta)^{1/2})$, and $\eta$.
A compatible corrected theorem form would use, for example, an upper bound by
$A(\eta^r+\delta^s)$ for universal $A\geq1$ and $r,s>0$.

\section{The source assertion}

Lemma~\texttt{lem:pasting} of \cite[lines~504--525]{Ji2020MIPStar} imports
Fact~4.35 from the NEEXP paper.  Its error is declared to be
$\operatorname{poly}(\eta,\delta)$.  The asymptotic convention in
\cite[lines~19--29]{Ji2020MIPStar} requires every variable to occur in the
product on the right side of the product contract.  The proof of the imported
fact, however, bounds the two sources of error independently; see
\cite[lines~1030--1209]{Natarajan2018NEEXP}.%
\footnote{The mirrored passages are
\path{references/qpbt-paper/04_preliminaries.tex}, lines~19--29;
\path{references/qpbt-paper/06_nonlocal_games_and_mipstar.tex}, lines~504--525;
and \path{references/neexp-paper/05_quantum_preliminaries.tex},
lines~1030--1209.}

More explicitly, fix a probability distribution $\mathcal D$ on
$(x,y_1,y_2)$, a unit bipartite state $\psi$, and nonnegative $\eta,\delta$.
Let $G_i^x$ be measurements indexed by functions
$g_i:\mathcal Y_i\to\mathcal R_i$, with $G_2^x$ projective, and let
$A^{x,y_1,y_2}$ be a projective joint answer measurement. Assume that distinct
second codewords agree with conditional probability at most $\eta$, given
$(x,y_1)$ whenever this conditioning has positive probability. Write
$Q_{i,a_i}=(G_i)^x_{[\operatorname{eval}_{y_i}(\cdot)=a_i]}$.
The input comparisons, averaged over $\mathcal D$, are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pasting-1}
 A^{x,y_1,y_2}_{a_i}\ot\mathrm{Id}
 \simeq_\delta\mathrm{Id}\ot Q_{i,a_i}\quad(i=1,2),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pasting-2}
 A^{x,y_1,y_2}_{a_1,a_2}\ot\mathrm{Id}
 \simeq_\delta\mathrm{Id}\ot A^{x,y_1,y_2}_{a_1,a_2}.
\end{equation}
Define $J^x_{g_1,g_2}=(G_2)^x_{g_2}(G_1)^x_{g_1}(G_2)^x_{g_2}$.
The asserted universal function $\delta_{\mathrm{pasting}}$ bounds
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pasting-3}
 A^{x,y_1,y_2}_{a_1,a_2}\ot\mathrm{Id}
 \simeq_{\delta_{\mathrm{pasting}}(\eta,\delta)}
 \mathrm{Id}\ot J^x_{[g_1(y_1)=a_1,\,g_2(y_2)=a_2]}.
\end{equation}
The error function is chosen before the distribution, alphabets, dimension,
state, and measurements; no constant may depend on these objects.

\section{A two-dimensional counterexample}

Fix $0<\rho<1$.  Let every question set, both codeword sets, and the second
answer set be a singleton, and let the first answer set be $\{0,1\}$.  Use the
point-mass question distribution and local Hilbert spaces $\C^2$.  In the
standard basis, take the normalized bipartite state
\[
  \ket{\psi_\rho}
    = \sqrt{1-\rho}\ket{0,0}+\sqrt{\rho}\ket{1,1}.
\]
Let both codeword measurements have their sole effect equal to the identity,
and evaluate the sole first codeword to $0$.  Let the joint answer measurement
be the standard-basis projective measurement: its effects at $(0,*)$ and
$(1,*)$ are respectively $\ket{0}\!\bra{0}$ and $\ket{1}\!\bra{1}$.

The second codeword set is a singleton, so its pairwise-collision hypothesis is
vacuous for every $\eta\geq0$.  The second marginal is exactly consistent, and
the perfectly correlated state makes the joint answer measurement exactly
self-consistent.  The first marginal differs from the constantly-zero
codeword answer precisely on the $\ket{1,1}$ component, so its consistency
defect is $\rho$.  Consequently all hypotheses of the pasting lemma hold with
$\delta=\rho$.

The pasted measurement also has a single codeword outcome and its effect is the
identity.  After evaluation it is deterministic at $(0,*)$, whereas the joint
answer measurement returns $(1,*)$ with probability $\rho$.  Their final
consistency defect is therefore $\rho$.  Thus the asserted conclusion would
require
\[
  \rho \leq \delta_{\mathrm{pasting}}(\eta,\rho)
  \qquad\text{for every }\eta>0.
\]
The product contract gives the opposite asymptotic bound
$\delta_{\mathrm{pasting}}(\eta,\rho)\leq C(\eta\rho)^C$.  Since $C>0$, the
right side tends to zero with $\eta$, which is a contradiction.  As $\rho$ can
be arbitrarily small, this failure is not confined to the large-error boundary.

\section{Status of the one-sided formal statement}

\subsection*{Source-domain audit}

The definition of a symmetric strategy in
\cite[lines~84--86]{Ji2020MIPStar} requires a swap-invariant state and identical
measurements. The sentence at lines~174--176 says that attention is
\emph{almost always} restricted to symmetric strategies. Neither passage
imposes this hypothesis on every subsequent operator lemma. In particular,
\texttt{lem:pasting}, lines~504--525, states the two forward marginal
comparisons and the self-consistency of the joint answer measurement, not
swap invariance or a fourth comparison. The earlier assertion in this note
that the one-sided form was unattested is withdrawn.

The imported Fact~4.35, \texttt{fact:low-degree-sandwich-on-steroids} in
\cite[lines~1000--1030]{Natarajan2018NEEXP}, likewise states the forward
comparisons and joint self-consistency. Its move at lines~1150--1185 requires
consistency of the second codeword family with its identical opposite-register
copy. The invocation of the triangle estimate does not justify this from the
three printed comparisons. This invalidates that particular proof step, not
necessarily its conclusion.

Indeed, the extra comparison need not follow even when $\eta=\delta=0$.
Take one question of each kind, $\mathcal H=\mathbb C^2$, and
$\psi=|0\rangle\ot|1\rangle$. Let the first answer alphabet have one element,
let $G_1=\mathrm{Id}$, and let the two second codewords have different values
$0,1$ at the unique second question. Set $G_{2,0}=|0\rangle\langle0|$,
$G_{2,1}=|1\rangle\langle1|$. Let $A$ always answer $(\ast,1)$:
$A_{\ast,1}=\mathrm{Id}$ and $A_{\ast,0}=0$.
The conditional collision probability and all three printed defects are zero.
The defect of the register-exchanged second comparison is one. Nevertheless
$J_{\ast,b}=G_{2,b}$, so the conclusion also has defect zero. Thus this is a
counterexample to deriving the extra comparison, \emph{not} to the one-sided
pasting theorem. The previously documented counterexample to the product
error contract is separate and remains valid.

\subsection*{A one-sided reduction}

Write $\psi_a=(A_a\ot\mathrm{Id})\psi$ for the post-measurement vectors of the
joint answer measurement, $P_{a_1}=(G_1)^x_{[\operatorname{eval}_{y_1}=a_1]}$,
$G_{g_2}=(G_2)^x_{g_2}$, and $[a_2]$ for the set of second codewords
evaluating to $a_2$ at $y_2$.  Since the first-factor projections are
orthogonal and commute with every second-factor operator, the defect of the
conclusion \eqref{eq:pasting-3} at a fixed question equals
\[
 \begin{aligned}
  &\sum_a\sum_{g_2\notin[a_2]}
    \bigl\|(\mathrm{Id}\ot G_{g_2})\psi_a\bigr\|^2\\
  &\quad+\sum_a\sum_{g_2\in[a_2]}
    \bigl\langle(\mathrm{Id}\ot G_{g_2})\psi_a\bigm|
      \mathrm{Id}\ot(\mathrm{Id}-P_{a_1})\bigm|
      (\mathrm{Id}\ot G_{g_2})\psi_a\bigr\rangle .
 \end{aligned}
\]
The first sum is the second-marginal defect of \eqref{eq:pasting-1}, at most
$\delta$ on average.  The second sum is at most the \emph{pinched defect}
\[
  \Delta_1=\sum_{a_1}\sum_{g_2}
    \bigl\langle(\mathrm{Id}\ot G_{g_2})\psi_{a_1}\bigm|
      \mathrm{Id}\ot(\mathrm{Id}-P_{a_1})\bigm|
      (\mathrm{Id}\ot G_{g_2})\psi_{a_1}\bigr\rangle,
\]
where $\psi_{a_1}=(A_{a_1}\ot\mathrm{Id})\psi$. This is the defect of the
first-marginal comparison against the family
$\sum_{g_2}G_{g_2}P_{a_1}G_{g_2}$.  The hypothesis \eqref{eq:pasting-1} for
$i=1$ bounds the same expression without the pinching by $G_2$.  The theorem
is therefore equivalent, up to an additive $\delta$, to the assertion that
measuring the second codeword family on the second factor does not destroy the
first-codeword mass of the vectors $\psi_{a_1}$.

\subsection*{Why the earlier estimates stopped}

Bounding each off-diagonal collision term in the pinched defect separately
loses a factor depending on the number of codewords. Assuming the
register-exchanged comparison avoids that loss, but the preceding example
shows that this is not a consequence of the source assumptions. It does not
show that such a restriction is necessary for the conclusion. The following
argument replaces the identical opposite-register copy by a constructed one.

\subsection*{A dimension-independent Schmidt-mirror argument}

This is an additional finite-dimensional argument, not a claimed passage of
the cited proof. Choose Schmidt bases once for the state and write
$\psi=\sum_i s_i e_i\ot f_i$, with $s_i\geq0$. The bases are completed across
the zero Schmidt coefficients. Put $S=\operatorname{diag}(s_i)$.
For a Bob operator $B$, define $\widetilde B$ on Alice to have matrix $B^T$
in these bases. Transposition preserves positivity, projectivity, sums, and
coarse-graining; hence a projective family is sent to a projective family.
This construction depends only on the state and on $B$, not on the sampled
second question. It does not change any operator in the conclusion.

For any Hermitian Alice operator $H$ and Hermitian Bob operator $B$, one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:schmidt-mirror}
 \| (\widetilde B\ot\mathrm{Id}-\mathrm{Id}\ot B)\psi\|^2
 \leq 2\| (H\ot\mathrm{Id}-\mathrm{Id}\ot B)\psi\|^2.
\end{equation}
Here is a proof that includes singular states and complex entries. For real
$s,t\geq0$ and complex $u,v$,
\begin{align*}
 |tu-sv|^2+|su-tv|^2-(s-t)^2|v|^2
   &=(s-t)^2|u|^2+2st|u-v|^2\geq0.
\end{align*}
Apply this with $u=H_{ij}$ and $v=(B^T)_{ij}$. Hermiticity identifies the two
terms on the left with the errors at $(i,j)$ and $(j,i)$ of $HS-SB^T$.
Summing over all ordered pairs gives
$\|B^TS-SB^T\|_F^2\leq2\|HS-SB^T\|_F^2$.
Vectorizing the coefficient matrix of $\psi$ proves
\eqref{eq:schmidt-mirror}. No inverse of $S$ or lower bound on its nonzero
entries is used. The scalar, diagonal-matrix, and Hilbert-norm estimates are
now kernel-checked as \leanid{schmidt\_pair\_mirror\_estimate},
\leanid{schmidt\_diagonal\_mirror\_estimate}, and
\leanid{schmidtMirror\_norm\_sq\_le}, respectively, in
\doclink{MIPStarRE/QPBT/Games/Sandwich/Pasting/SchmidtMirror.lean}.

For completeness, the formal construction obtains unitary $U,V$ and
$s_i\geq0$ with $C=U\operatorname{diag}(s_i)V^*$ for the coefficient matrix
$C$ of $\psi$. The Bob Schmidt basis consists of the conjugated columns of
$V$, so the mirror in the computational bases is $UV^*B^TVU^*$, not
$UB^TU^*$. The unitary choices are fixed once for $C$; they do not depend on
$B$, $H$, or any sampled question. The theorem
\leanid{exists\_schmidt\_coordinates} derives this decomposition by
reusing the project's proved positive-Gram completion: diagonalize $C^*C$,
normalize $Cv_i$ only when its eigenvalue is positive, and complete these
orthonormal images on the left. On zero-eigenvalue directions $Cv_i=0$, so
arbitrary orthonormal completion still gives the exact factorization.
The reused construction is in
\doclink{MIPStarRE/LDT/MakingMeasurementsProjective/QXPLayerIdentities/PositiveGram/Completion.lean};
its spectral input comes from
\doclink{.lake/packages/mathlib/Mathlib/Analysis/Matrix/Spectrum.lean}.
This is a derived representation of the state, not supplied proof data.

The linear map \leanid{schmidtMirror} preserves the identity, adjoints,
and positivity, and reverses multiplication. Consequently
\leanid{schmidtMirrorMeasurement} is a complete measurement, is projective
when the input is projective, and commutes with every coarse-graining.
These properties are proved in the same module. The norm estimate is stated
for every vector and every pair of Hermitian operators; it needs neither
normalization of the vector nor faithfulness of the reduced state.

Apply \eqref{eq:schmidt-mirror} to each coarse second-codeword projector
$Q_{a_2}$ and the corresponding Alice marginal $A_{a_2}$. For two projective
measurements on opposite registers, the summed squared distance is twice
the consistency defect. The second forward comparison therefore gives
\[
 \mathbb E\sum_{a_2}
 \|(\widetilde Q_{a_2}\ot\mathrm{Id}-\mathrm{Id}\ot Q_{a_2})\psi\|^2
 \leq4\delta.
\]
Let $\epsilon_c$ and $\epsilon_f$ denote the coarse and fine consistency
defects between the mirror family and the Bob family. Thus
$\epsilon_c\leq2\delta$. The positive weights
$\langle\psi|\widetilde G_h\ot G_g|\psi\rangle$ sum to one.
For $g\neq h$, split according to whether $g(y_2)=h(y_2)$.
After conditioning on $(x,y_1)$, the collision assumption bounds the agreeing
part by $\eta$, since these weights are independent of $y_2$.
The disagreeing part is the coarse defect. Consequently
\[
 \epsilon_f\leq\epsilon_c+\eta\leq2\delta+\eta,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E\sum_g\|(\widetilde G_g\ot\mathrm{Id}
                   -\mathrm{Id}\ot G_g)\psi\|^2\leq4\delta+2\eta.
\]
Zero-probability conditional fibers contribute zero and need no assumption.

The two forward comparisons give the coarse commutator estimate
$K_0\leq C\delta$ for a universal $C\geq1$, by the source's joint-projective
commutation calculus, formalized in \leanid{exists\_coarse\_commutator\_bound}.
The ordered-product mass estimate \leanid{mass\_ordered\_product\_ge} gives
an averaged mass at least $1-m$, where
\[
 m=2\sqrt{2\delta}+2\delta+2\sqrt{C\delta}+C\delta.
\]
This is the explicit bound used in Lean; neither estimate requires register
exchange. For the cross terms, replace every Alice occurrence of $G_g$ by
$\widetilde G_g$ and of $Q_{a_2}$ by $\widetilde Q_{a_2}$.
The fiber expansion uses linearity and compatibility with coarse-graining,
which the mirror preserves. In particular, the coarse pinched cross term
minus the fine one becomes
\[
 \sum_{a_1}\sum_{g\neq h,\ g(y_2)=h(y_2)}
 \langle\psi|\widetilde G_h\ot P_{a_1}G_gP_{a_1}|\psi\rangle.
\]
Each term is nonnegative. Summing without the collision condition is at most
$\sum_{a_1}\langle\psi|\mathrm{Id}\ot P_{a_1}^2|\psi\rangle\leq1$.
Its conditional average is therefore at most $\eta$, with no codeword-count
factor. Expanding the four commutator terms, the reversed-product masses
agree, the ordered-product masses differ by at most $m$, and Cauchy--Schwarz
in the two cross moves gives
\[
 K\leq C\delta+m+2\eta+2\sqrt{4\delta+2\eta}+2\sqrt{4\delta}.
\]
This is the distinct-family estimate
\leanid{commutator\_mass\_fine\_le\_distinct\_families} applied to the
constructed mirror. The pinched reduction bounds the final defect by
$2\delta+\sqrt K$.

For the final scalar calculation on $[0,1]^2$, Lean uses the convenient
upper bound $2\delta\leq t=\delta+2\sqrt{2\delta}$ in the coarse mirror
consistency estimate. It therefore bounds $K$ by
\[
 \widehat K=C\delta+m+2\eta+2\sqrt{2(t+\eta)}+2\sqrt{2t}.
\]
Writing $u=\delta^{1/8}$ and $v=\eta^{1/4}$, the existing scalar estimate
\leanid{pasting\_error\_sqrt\_le\_rpow} bounds
$\widehat K$ by $(3C+18)u^2+6v^2$ and yields
\[
 2\delta+\sqrt{\widehat K}\leq(3C+19)(\eta^{1/4}+\delta^{1/8}).
\]
Outside the unit square the same error function exceeds one, which bounds
every consistency defect between opposite-register measurements of a unit
vector. Thus the universal error function can be chosen, before any types,
state, distribution, or measurements, as
\[
 \delta_{\mathrm{pasting}}(\eta,\delta)
   =(3C+19)(\eta^{1/4}+\delta^{1/8}).
\]
The joint self-consistency assumption is not needed by this argument but is
retained in the public statement. No state symmetry, faithful reduced state,
or reversed marginal comparison is assumed.

\subsection*{Adversarial checks and completed formal argument}

The argument permits zero Schmidt coefficients, arbitrarily unequal positive
coefficients, complex Hermitian effects (the mirror is a transpose, not an
identical copy), a nonprojective first family, arbitrary finite codeword
cardinality, and correlated question distributions. At $\delta=0$ it gives
an error tending to zero with $\eta$; at $\eta=0$ it gives an error tending to
zero with $\delta$. For the product-state example above, the mirror of
$G_{2,1}$ is the projection onto Alice's Schmidt partner $|0\rangle$, not the
original matrix $|1\rangle\langle1|$. Thus the construction does not purport
to derive the disproved extra comparison.

The public theorem \leanid{exists\_pasting\_error} now has a complete proof
from its source-aligned hypotheses. Its former single proof hole is removed.
The averaged coarse estimate is
\leanid{consistencyDefect\_schmidtMirror\_le}; it holds even for an
unnormalized vector and a nonprojective Alice predictor. Projectivity is
required only for the Bob family, so that the mirror and Bob distances equal
twice their consistency defect. Averaging uses nonnegative distribution
weights and introduces no dimension-dependent factor.

The independently proved cross-move and collision estimates are integrated
through \leanid{pinched\_coarse\_sub\_fine\_eq\_distinct\_families} and
\leanid{commutator\_mass\_fine\_le\_distinct\_families}.
Their identical-family versions remain proved specializations with unchanged
signatures. The final analytic estimate is
\leanid{consistencyDefect\_pasted\_le\_sqrt\_one\_sided}; it takes the
two forward comparisons, not a fourth comparison or self-consistency.
Its unit-interval restriction is used only to reuse a conservative scalar
majorant and is discharged by the trivial bound outside that interval in
\leanid{exists\_pasting\_error}. It is not a restriction of the public theorem.

The separate conditional result
\leanid{exists\_pasting\_error\_of\_register\_exchange} still assumes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pasting-1-sym}
 (G_2)^x_{[\operatorname{eval}_{y_2}(\cdot)=a_2]}\ot\mathrm{Id}
 \simeq_\delta\mathrm{Id}\ot A^{x,y_1,y_2}_{a_2}.
\end{equation}
It is not used by the source-facing theorem. Its earlier proposed use as a
source-domain correction is withdrawn: the mirror proof shows that no
additional operator hypothesis is necessary. The original forward hypotheses
are weaker than either register-exchange or state-symmetry restrictions and
are sufficient. No mathematical definition or game specification is changed.

\subsection*{Proof of the conditional register-exchange statement}


Fix a question $(x,y_1,y_2)$ and keep the notation of the reduction above,
with $Q_{a_2}=(G_2)^x_{[\operatorname{eval}_{y_2}=a_2]}$.  Write
$\delta_1,\delta_2,\delta_3\le\delta$ for the defects of the three forward
comparisons and $\delta_2'\le\delta$ for that of \eqref{eq:pasting-1-sym};
averages over the question are taken at the end, with Jensen's inequality for
the square roots.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Reduction.}  By the reduction above, the defect of
  \eqref{eq:pasting-3} is at most $\delta_2+\Delta_1$.
  \item \emph{From the pinched defect to a commutator.}  Since
  $\sum_{g_2}G_{g_2}=\mathrm{Id}$, the quantity $1-\Delta_1$ differs from
  $\sum_{a_1}\langle\psi|A_{a_1}\ot P_{a_1}|\psi\rangle=1-\delta_1$ by
  $\sum_{a_1,g_2}\langle\psi|A_{a_1}\ot[G_{g_2},P_{a_1}]G_{g_2}|\psi\rangle$,
  and Cauchy--Schwarz bounds this by $\sqrt K$, where
  $K=\sum_{a_1,g_2}\|\,\mathrm{Id}\ot[P_{a_1},G_{g_2}]\,\psi\|^2$.  Hence
  $\Delta_1\le\delta_1+\sqrt K$.
  \item \emph{Coarse commutators.}  The comparisons \eqref{eq:pasting-1}
  move $P_{a_1}$ and $Q_{a_2}$ one at a time onto the joint projective
  measurement on the first factor: in the summed square norm,
  $(\mathrm{Id}\ot P_{a_1}Q_{a_2})\psi$ and
  $(\mathrm{Id}\ot Q_{a_2}P_{a_1})\psi$ are both within $2\sqrt{2\delta}$ of
  $(A_{a_1,a_2}\ot\mathrm{Id})\psi$.  Thus
  $K_0=\sum_{a_1,a_2}\|\,\mathrm{Id}\ot[P_{a_1},Q_{a_2}]\,\psi\|^2\le32\delta$
  and $\sum_{a_1,a_2}\|(\mathrm{Id}\ot P_{a_1}Q_{a_2})\psi\|^2\ge
  1-4\sqrt{2\delta}$ when $8\delta\le1$ (larger values are absorbed by the
  constant, the defect being at most $1$).  This step is one-sided.
  \item \emph{Cross consistency of $G_2$.}  From \eqref{eq:pasting-1-sym},
  the marginal of the self-consistency \eqref{eq:pasting-2}, and the second
  comparison in \eqref{eq:pasting-1}, the triangle estimate for consistency
  gives
  $\epsilon_c=\sum_{a_2\neq a_2'}\langle\psi|Q_{a_2}\ot Q_{a_2'}|\psi\rangle
  \le\delta+2\sqrt{2\delta}$.  Splitting the pairs $g_2\neq g_2'$ according
  to whether $g_2(y_2)=g_2'(y_2)$, the collision bound and the positivity of
  $\langle\psi|G_{g_2}\ot G_{g_2'}|\psi\rangle$ give, on average over $y_2$,
  $\epsilon_f=\sum_{g_2\neq g_2'}\langle\psi|G_{g_2}\ot G_{g_2'}|\psi\rangle
  \le\epsilon_c+\eta$.  Both families being projective,
  $\sum_{g_2}\|(G_{g_2}\ot\mathrm{Id}-\mathrm{Id}\ot G_{g_2})\psi\|^2
  =2\epsilon_f$, and likewise $2\epsilon_c$ for the coarse family.  This is
  the only step using \eqref{eq:pasting-1-sym}.
  \item \emph{Fine commutators.}  Expand $K$ and $K_0$ into four terms each
  and compare term by term.  The first fine term is at most $1$ and the first
  coarse term is at least $1-4\sqrt{2\delta}$ by step 3.  The second terms
  agree, both being $\sum_{a_1}\langle\psi|\mathrm{Id}\ot P_{a_1}^2|\psi\rangle$.
  In the two cross terms, written with the vectors
  $(\mathrm{Id}\ot G_{g_2}P_{a_1})\psi$ on the left, the rightmost effect is
  moved to the first factor at cost $\sqrt{2\epsilon_f}$ (fine) and
  $\sqrt{2\epsilon_c}$ (coarse); after the move the coarse expression exceeds
  the fine one by
  $\sum_{a_1}\sum_{g_2\neq g_2',\,g_2(y_2)=g_2'(y_2)}
  \langle\psi|G_{g_2'}\ot P_{a_1}G_{g_2}P_{a_1}|\psi\rangle$, a sum of
  expectations of tensor products of positive operators, which is
  nonnegative and, by the collision bound, at most $\eta$ on average.
  Altogether
  $K\le K_0+4\sqrt{2\delta}+2\eta+2\sqrt{2\epsilon_f}+2\sqrt{2\epsilon_c}$.
\end{enumerate}
Combining, the averaged defect of \eqref{eq:pasting-3} is at most
$2\delta+\sqrt K$ with $K$ bounded by a universal sum of positive powers of
$\delta$ and $\eta$, so $\delta_{\mathrm{pasting}}$ satisfies the corrected
contract of the previous sections.  Explicitly, with $C$ the constant of the
coarse commutator bound of step 3, one has
$K\le(3C+18)\delta^{1/4}+6\sqrt\eta$ on $[0,1]^2$, whence
$2\delta+\sqrt K\le(3C+19)(\eta^{1/4}+\delta^{1/8})$ there; outside the unit
square the same function exceeds $1$, which bounds every defect of two placed
families.  The formalization of this conditional argument is complete (\ghissue{201});
it is not the proof of the one-sided theorem, which is now independently
proved by the mirror argument above.

\section{Conclusion}

The literal product-form interpretation of
$\operatorname{poly}(\eta,\delta)$ makes the stated pasting lemma false.  This
is a theorem-level scalar discrepancy, not a missing formal-library lemma.  The
natural repair is to adopt a multivariate polynomial-smallness convention that
allows separate positive powers of the input errors, matching the terms in the
imported proof.  Adding an unadvertised coupling hypothesis between $\eta$ and
$\delta$ would also avoid the counterexample, but would strengthen the source
theorem and is therefore not the preferred repair.
