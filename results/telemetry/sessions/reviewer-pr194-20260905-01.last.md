## Review
F1: The blueprint defines \(\chi(s)\) at lines 41–46 by
\[
s=(\chi(s)-1)q/m+r,
\]
so \(\chi(s)\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\). By contrast, `chiIndex` at `MIPStarRE/QPBT/Test/LowDegreeGame.lean:111` has value \(\lfloor s/(q/m)\rfloor\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}\). Therefore lines 703–708 and 723–730 should identify the first component with \(\chi_0(s)=\chi(s)-1\), not \(\chi(s)\). Introduce that notation explicitly and relate it to the paper convention; until then, both affected statement-level `\leanok` marks are inaccurate.
F2: `aLinePointDist` at `MIPStarRE/QPBT/Observables/LineDefs.lean:165` is a push-forward through `aLineDescOf`, and its `LineDesc.axis` outcome retains the scalar seed. The blueprint instead calls it the raw CL distribution over geometric lines \((u_0,e_i)\), dropping \(s\). Since every index has \(q/m\) seeds, this is not an equivalence of outcome spaces. Either formalize the source’s geometric-line push-forward, or leave the source-labelled definition unmarked and record the seed-bearing law separately as a formalization-only variant.
F3: `uniformDistribution_map_fst_of_equiv` at `MIPStarRE/QPBT/Games/DistributionMarginals.lean:58` assumes that \(A,B,C\) are finite and nonempty. In particular, nonemptiness of \(C\) is load-bearing for the first projection. State “For nonempty finite sets \(A,B,C\)” before introducing \(E\); the decidable-equality instances may remain implicit boundary data.
F4: Add `def:ld-question-distribution` to line 702 because that definition introduces \(\chi\). The existing dependencies only provide the parameters and binary representation.
F5: Replace the implementation description with mathematical prose, for example: “A finite distribution is determined by its support and its weight function.”
F6: Replace `lem:prefix-projection-idem` with the actual label `lem:prefix-projection-idempotent`.
Summary: 3 equivalence mismatches affecting 4 statement-level `\leanok` marks, 1 dependency omission, 1 stale cross-reference, and 1 prose issue. After correction, the next review should recheck the line-point statements against the chosen zero-based notation and decoder convention.
