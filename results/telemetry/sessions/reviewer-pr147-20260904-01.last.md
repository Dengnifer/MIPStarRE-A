## Review
F1 is the principal mathematical blocker. In `references/ldt-paper/test_definition.tex:49-65`, the mature diagonal test samples an index \(i\), restricts the last \(m-i\) direction coordinates to zero, and reveals only the line to the prover. Conversely, `references/qpbt-paper/08_classical_and_quantum_low_degree_tests.tex:230-237`, equation `eq:cl-dlnf`, uses a revealed index and zeroes the first \(i-1\) coordinates. The required averaging and coordinate alignment are explicitly identified in `docs/paper-gaps/qpbt_ld-dimension-divisibility.tex:213-230`.
Hard-coding direct index zero neither averages over the revealed direct indices nor aligns these restriction conventions. A direct strategy can misbehave only at index zero when the last direction coordinate vanishes, an \(O(1/(mq))\) event in the direct law, while the constructed mature strategy encounters that condition for every restricted index below \(m\), with constant probability. Thus the Born-weight identities cannot yield the required value bound. This needs a genuine de-indexing/averaging construction and corresponding distribution bound.
F2 is an A3-style zero-fallback. The direct predicate at `Game.lean:141-148` accepts a zero-direction answer when its polynomial function is constant with the matching point value. `blueprint/src/chapter/ch13_qpbt_test.tex:120-121`, remark `rem:ld-win-zero-direction`, specifically says that this constant is \(g(u)\), not necessarily zero. A perfect constant-\(c\) strategy with \(c\ne0\) is therefore mapped to line output zero and point output \(c\). The right-form answer should be mapped to the translation-invariant constant polynomial carrying its evaluated value, or an explicit additive zero-direction loss must be proved.
